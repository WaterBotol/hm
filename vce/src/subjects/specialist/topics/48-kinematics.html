Position, velocity, acceleration

Calculus links
\[ v = \frac{dx}{dt}, \qquad a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{d^2x}{dt^2}, \qquad x = \int v\,dt, \qquad v = \int a\,dt \]

Displacement \(= \int_{t_1}^{t_2} v\,dt\) (signed). Distance \(= \int_{t_1}^{t_2} |v|\,dt\): split wherever \(v\) changes sign. "At rest" means \(v = 0\); "changes direction" means \(v\) changes sign.

A particle moves with \(v = t^2 - 4t + 3\) m/s for \(0 \le t \le 4\). Find its displacement and the distance travelled.

\(v = (t - 1)(t - 3)\), so it changes direction at \(t = 1\) and \(t = 3\). Split the distance integral there.

\(x(t) - x(0) = \frac{t^3}{3} - 2t^2 + 3t\); values at \(t = 1, 3, 4\): \(\frac43,\ 0,\ \frac43\).

Displacement \(= \frac43\) m. Distance \(= \frac43 + \left|0 - \frac43\right| + \frac43\).

Displacement \(\frac43\) m; distance \(4\) m.

Velocity–time graphs and constant acceleration

On a \(v\)–\(t\) graph: gradient = acceleration, signed area = displacement, total area = distance.

Constant acceleration
\[ v = u + at \qquad s = ut + \tfrac12at^2 \qquad v^2 = u^2 + 2as \qquad s = \tfrac12(u + v)t \qquad s = vt - \tfrac12at^2 \]

Only valid when \(a\) is constant. Otherwise use calculus.

A car travelling at 25 m/s brakes uniformly and stops in 50 m. Find the deceleration and stopping time.

Known: \(u = 25\), \(v = 0\), \(s = 50\). Use \(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\).

\(0 = 625 + 100a \Rightarrow a = -6.25\) m/s².

Deceleration 6.25 m/s²; \(t = \frac{v - u}{a} = 4\) s.

Acceleration in other forms

Forms of acceleration
\[ a = \frac{dv}{dt} = v\frac{dv}{dx} = \frac{d}{dx}\left(\tfrac12 v^2\right) \]

Choose by what \(a\) depends on: \(a(t)\) → \(\frac{dv}{dt}\); \(a(x)\) → \(\frac{d}{dx}(\frac12v^2)\); \(a(v)\) → \(\frac{dv}{dt}\) (to find \(t\)) or \(v\frac{dv}{dx}\) (to find \(x\)).

A particle has \(a = -4x\), and \(v = 6\) when \(x = 0\). Find \(v\) in terms of \(x\).

\(a\) is a function of \(x\), so use \(\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac12v^2\right) = -4x\) and integrate with respect to \(x\).

\(\frac12v^2 = -2x^2 + c\); \(x = 0, v = 6 \Rightarrow c = 18\).

\(v^2 = 36 - 4x^2\), so \(v = \pm\sqrt{36 - 4x^2}\), and the particle oscillates between \(x = \pm3\).

A boat moving at 10 m/s cuts its engine and decelerates with \(a = -0.5v\). Find (a) \(v\) in terms of \(t\), and (b) how far it travels before stopping.

(a) \(\frac{dv}{dt} = -0.5v\): exponential decay. (b) For distance, use \(v\frac{dv}{dx} = -0.5v\), so \(\frac{dv}{dx} = -0.5\).

(a) \(v = 10e^{-0.5t}\).

(b) \(v = 10 - 0.5x\), so \(v = 0\) at \(x = 20\).

(a) \(v = 10e^{-t/2}\); (b) 20 m (in the limit as \(t \to \infty\)).

Don't use constant acceleration formulas when \(a\) varies. And when \(a\) depends on \(v\), you can't integrate \(\frac{dv}{dt} = f(v)\) with respect to \(t\) directly: flip it to \(\frac{dt}{dv} = \frac{1}{f(v)}\).

Combined with forces from Unit 3/4 contexts, VCAA asks about falling objects with air resistance: \(a = g - kv^2\). Terminal velocity is where \(a = 0\): \(v_T = \sqrt{g/k}\). Give units and directions (state which way is positive).

Practice

A particle has \(x = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t\). It is at rest when \(t =\)

  1. 0 and 3
  2. 2 only
  3. 1 and 3
  4. 3 only

\(v = 3t^2 - 12t + 9 = 3(t - 1)(t - 3)\).

A stone is thrown up at 19.6 m/s (\(g = 9.8\)). Its maximum height is

  1. 9.8 m
  2. 19.6 m
  3. 39.2 m
  4. 2 m

\(0 = 19.6^2 - 2(9.8)s \Rightarrow s = 19.6\) m.

If \(v = x^2 + 1\), then the acceleration when \(x = 1\) is

  1. 4
  2. 2
  3. 3
  4. 8

\(a = v\frac{dv}{dx} = (x^2 + 1)(2x) = 2\times2 = 4\).

A body has \(v = 2 - t\) m/s. The distance travelled in the first 4 s is

  1. 0 m
  2. 8 m
  3. 2 m
  4. 4 m

Two triangles of area 2 either side of \(t = 2\): displacement 0, distance 4.

A particle starts from rest at \(x = 0\) with \(a = 6t - 2\). Find \(x\) when \(t = 2\).

\(v = 3t^2 - 2t\) (\(v(0) = 0\)). [1]

\(x = t^3 - t^2\) (\(x(0) = 0\)). [1]

\(x(2) = 4\) m. [1]

A particle falling from rest has \(a = 10 - 0.1v^2\) (down positive). Find its terminal velocity, and an expression for the distance fallen when its speed is \(v\).

Terminal: \(0 = 10 - 0.1v^2 \Rightarrow v_T = 10\) m/s. [1]

\(v\frac{dv}{dx} = 10 - 0.1v^2 \Rightarrow \frac{dx}{dv} = \frac{v}{10 - 0.1v^2} = \frac{10v}{100 - v^2}\). [1]

\(x = -5\log_e(100 - v^2) + c\); \(x = 0\) at \(v = 0\) gives \(c = 5\log_e 100\). [1]

\(x = 5\log_e\dfrac{100}{100 - v^2}\), \(0 \le v < 10\). [1]

A particle moves with \(a = -\frac{1}{x^2}\) for \(x > 0\), and \(v = 2\) when \(x = 1\). Find \(v^2\) in terms of \(x\), and the largest \(x\) it reaches.

\(\frac12v^2 = \frac1x + c\). [1]

\(2 = 1 + c \Rightarrow c = 1\), so \(v^2 = \frac2x + 2\). [1]

\(v^2 > 2\) for all \(x > 0\), so it never stops: no maximum (it escapes, approaching speed \(\sqrt2\)). [1]

Summary

  • \(v = \dot x\), \(a = \dot v\); displacement \(\int v\), distance \(\int|v|\) (split at sign changes).
  • Constant \(a\) only: \(v = u + at\), \(s = ut + \frac12at^2\), \(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\).
  • \(a = v\frac{dv}{dx} = \frac{d}{dx}(\frac12v^2)\) when \(a\) depends on \(x\) (or \(v\), to find distance).
  • Terminal velocity: set \(a = 0\).
