Areas between curves

Upper minus lower
\[ A = \int_a^b \big(f(x) - g(x)\big)\,dx \quad \text{where } f(x) \ge g(x) \text{ on } [a, b] \]

Find the intersections for the terminals. If the curves cross inside the interval, split the integral. Area is always positive.

Find the area enclosed by \(y = x^2\) and \(y = 2x + 3\).

Solve for the intersections, decide which graph is on top (test a point between), then integrate top minus bottom.

\(x^2 = 2x + 3 \Rightarrow (x - 3)(x + 1) = 0\): \(x = -1, 3\). At \(x = 0\) the line is higher.

\(A = \int_{-1}^{3} (2x + 3 - x^2)\,dx = \left[x^2 + 3x - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^{3}\).

\(= (9 + 9 - 9) - (1 - 3 + \frac13) = 9 + \frac53 = \frac{32}{3}\).

Find the area bounded by \(y = \log_e x\), the \(y\)-axis, and the lines \(y = 0\) and \(y = 1\).

The region sits between the \(y\)-axis and the curve, so it's easier as horizontal strips: \(x = e^y\), integrated from \(y = 0\) to \(y = 1\).

\(A = \int_0^1 e^y\,dy\).

\(= e - 1\).

Volumes of revolution

Discs
\[ \text{about the } x\text{-axis: } V = \pi\int_a^b y^2\,dx \qquad \text{about the } y\text{-axis: } V = \pi\int_c^d x^2\,dy \]
Washers (region between two curves)
\[ V = \pi\int_a^b \big(y_{\text{outer}}^2 - y_{\text{inner}}^2\big)\,dx \]

Square each radius separately: it's \(\pi(R^2 - r^2)\), never \(\pi(R - r)^2\).

The region under \(y = \sqrt{x}\) from \(x = 0\) to \(x = 4\) is rotated about the \(x\)-axis. Find the volume.

Each slice is a disc of radius \(y = \sqrt x\) and thickness \(dx\).

\(V = \pi\int_0^4 x\,dx = \pi\left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^4\).

\(= 8\pi\) cubic units.

The region bounded by \(y = x^2\), the \(y\)-axis and \(y = 4\) (for \(x \ge 0\)) is rotated about the \(y\)-axis. Find the volume.

Rotating about the \(y\)-axis uses horizontal discs of radius \(x\), so write \(x^2\) in terms of \(y\) and integrate with \(y\) terminals.

\(x^2 = y\): \(V = \pi\int_0^4 y\,dy\).

\(= \pi\left[\frac{y^2}{2}\right]_0^4 = 8\pi\).

The region between \(y = x\) and \(y = x^2\) is rotated about the \(x\)-axis. Find the volume.

They meet at \(x = 0\) and \(x = 1\). On \((0, 1)\), \(x > x^2\), so \(y = x\) is the outer radius and \(y = x^2\) the inner.

\(V = \pi\int_0^1 (x^2 - x^4)\,dx\).

\(= \pi\left[\frac{x^3}{3} - \frac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^1 = \pi\left(\frac13 - \frac15\right)\).

\(= \frac{2\pi}{15}\).

Two common errors: (1) \(\pi\int(y_1 - y_2)^2dx\) instead of \(\pi\int(y_1^2 - y_2^2)dx\); (2) rotating about the \(y\)-axis but integrating \(y^2\,dx\). Match the axis: about \(x\) → \(dx\) and \(y^2\); about \(y\) → \(dy\) and \(x^2\).

Exam 2 often asks you to "write down a definite integral that gives the volume" (marks for the integral alone, then CAS for the value). Write \(\pi\), the squared function, and correct terminals explicitly. Real-world versions: a vase, bowl or wine glass, with units and sometimes a rate of filling (combining with related rates).

Practice

The area between \(y = \sin x\) and the \(x\)-axis from \(x = 0\) to \(x = 2\pi\) is

  1. 0
  2. 4
  3. 2
  4. \(2\pi\)

Split at \(\pi\): \(2 + |-2| = 4\). The integral alone gives 0.

The region under \(y = e^x\) from \(x = 0\) to \(x = 1\) is rotated about the \(x\)-axis. The volume is

  1. \(\frac{\pi}{2}(e^2 - 1)\)
  2. \(\pi(e - 1)\)
  3. \(\pi(e^2 - 1)\)
  4. \(\frac\pi2 e^2\)

\(\pi\int_0^1 e^{2x}dx = \frac\pi2(e^2 - 1)\).

The region between \(y = 2\) and \(y = 1\) for \(0 \le x \le 3\) is rotated about the \(x\)-axis. The volume is

  1. \(3\pi\)
  2. \(\pi\)
  3. \(12\pi\)
  4. \(9\pi\)

Washer: \(\pi\int_0^3 (4 - 1)dx = 9\pi\). Using \((2 - 1)^2\) gives the wrong answer A.

The curve \(y = \sqrt{4 - x^2}\) for \(-2 \le x \le 2\) is rotated about the \(x\)-axis. The volume is

  1. \(8\pi\)
  2. \(\frac{16\pi}{3}\)
  3. \(\frac{32\pi}{3}\)
  4. \(4\pi\)

A sphere of radius 2: \(\pi\int_{-2}^2(4 - x^2)dx = \frac{32\pi}{3} = \frac43\pi(2)^3\).

Find the area enclosed by \(y = x^2 - 4\) and \(y = 0\).

Intersections \(x = \pm2\); the curve is below the axis between them. [1]

\(A = \int_{-2}^{2}(0 - (x^2 - 4))\,dx = \left[4x - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-2}^{2}\). [1]

\(= \frac{16}{3} - \left(-\frac{16}{3}\right) = \frac{32}{3}\). [1]

The region bounded by \(y = \sec x\), the \(x\)-axis, \(x = 0\) and \(x = \frac\pi4\) is rotated about the \(x\)-axis. Find the exact volume.

\(V = \pi\int_0^{\pi/4}\sec^2 x\,dx\). [1]

\(= \pi\big[\tan x\big]_0^{\pi/4}\). [1]

\(= \pi\). [1]

A bowl is formed by rotating \(y = x^2\) for \(0 \le x \le 2\) about the \(y\)-axis (units cm). Find its volume when filled to depth \(h\) cm, and the volume when full.

About the \(y\)-axis: \(x^2 = y\). [1]

\(V(h) = \pi\int_0^h y\,dy = \frac{\pi h^2}{2}\). [1]

Full: \(y\) at \(x = 2\) is 4, so \(h = 4\). [1]

\(V = 8\pi \approx 25.1\) cm³. [1]

Summary

  • Area: \(\int(\text{top} - \text{bottom})\,dx\), or right minus left in \(dy\); split where curves cross.
  • About the \(x\)-axis: \(\pi\int y^2\,dx\); about the \(y\)-axis: \(\pi\int x^2\,dy\).
  • Washers: \(\pi\int(R^2 - r^2)\), never \(\pi\int(R - r)^2\).
