Building the interval

Approximate C% confidence interval for μ
\[ \left(\bar x - z\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n},\ \bar x + z\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}\right), \qquad z = 1.6449\ (90\%),\ 1.96\ (95\%),\ 2.5758\ (99\%) \]

If \(\sigma\) is unknown and \(n\) is large, use the sample sd \(s\) in its place. The margin of error is \(E = z\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}\) (half the width).

A random sample of 50 lightbulbs has mean lifetime 1210 h. The population sd is 80 h. Find an approximate 95% confidence interval for the mean lifetime.

\(\bar x \pm 1.96\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}\).

\(1.96\times\frac{80}{\sqrt{50}} = 1.96\times11.314 \approx 22.17\).

\((1187.8,\ 1232.2)\) hours, to 1 d.p.

A 95% CI for \(\mu\) is \((24.1, 27.9)\). Find \(\bar x\) and the margin of error.

The interval is symmetric about \(\bar x\).

\(\bar x = 26.0\); \(E = 1.9\).

With \(\sigma = 12\), how large a sample is needed for a 99% CI with margin of error at most 2?

Solve \(2.5758\frac{12}{\sqrt n} \le 2\) and round up.

\(\sqrt n \ge \frac{2.5758\times12}{2} = 15.455\), \(n \ge 238.9\).

\(n = 239\).

Interpreting correctly

"95% confidence" describes the method: if we took many samples and built an interval from each, about 95% of the intervals would contain \(\mu\). For one computed interval, \(\mu\) is fixed: it's either in it or not.

Wrong: "there's a 95% chance \(\mu\) is in \((1187.8, 1232.2)\)". Also wrong: "95% of bulbs last between 1187.8 and 1232.2 h": the interval is about the mean, not individual values.

Common Exam 2 parts: find a CI, decide whether a claimed value is "plausible" (inside the interval), and "of 20 such intervals, how many expected to contain \(\mu\)?" (binomial: \(20\times0.95 = 19\)). Higher confidence → wider; larger \(n\) → narrower.

Summary

  • CI: \(\bar x \pm z\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}\), with \(z = 1.6449, 1.96, 2.5758\) for 90, 95, 99%.
  • Interpret as a long-run capture rate of the method, about \(\mu\) (not individual values).
  • Width ↑ with confidence, ↓ with \(\sqrt n\). Sample size: solve and round up.
