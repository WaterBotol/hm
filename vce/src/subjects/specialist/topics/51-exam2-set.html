Section A: multiple choice

1. The negation of "for all real \(x\), \(x^2 \ge x\)" is

  1. for all real \(x\), \(x^2 < x\)
  2. there exists real \(x\) with \(x^2 \ge x\)
  3. for all real \(x\), \(x^2 \le x\)
  4. there exists real \(x\) with \(x^2 < x\)
  5. there is no real \(x\) with \(x^2 < x\)

Negation swaps \(\forall\) to \(\exists\) and negates the inequality. (It's true: \(x = \frac12\).)

2. The maximal domain of \(f(x) = \sin^{-1}(2x - 1)\) is

  1. \([0, 1]\)
  2. \([-1, 1]\)
  3. \([0, 2]\)
  4. \(\left[-\frac12, \frac12\right]\)
  5. \((0, 1)\)

\(-1 \le 2x - 1 \le 1 \Rightarrow 0 \le x \le 1\).

3. The asymptotes of \(y = \dfrac{x^2 + 1}{x - 1}\) are

  1. \(x = 1\) and \(y = 1\)
  2. \(x = 1\) and \(y = x\)
  3. \(x = 1\) and \(y = x + 1\)
  4. \(x = -1\) and \(y = x - 1\)
  5. \(x = 1\) only

\(\frac{x^2 + 1}{x - 1} = x + 1 + \frac{2}{x - 1}\).

4. \(\operatorname{Arg}\big((-1 + i)^5\big)\) is

  1. \(\frac{15\pi}{4}\)
  2. \(-\frac\pi4\)
  3. \(\frac{3\pi}{4}\)
  4. \(\frac\pi4\)
  5. \(-\frac{3\pi}{4}\)

\(5\times\frac{3\pi}{4} = \frac{15\pi}{4}\), then subtract \(4\pi\) to land in \((-\pi, \pi]\).

5. Euler's method with step 0.5 is applied to \(\frac{dy}{dx} = \sqrt{x + y}\), \(y(0) = 1\). The estimate of \(y(1)\) is closest to

  1. 2.207
  2. 2.000
  3. 2.250
  4. 1.500
  5. 2.414

\(y_1 = 1 + 0.5(1) = 1.5\); \(y_2 = 1.5 + 0.5\sqrt{2} \approx 2.207\).

6. The region inside \(\frac{x^2}{9} + \frac{y^2}{4} = 1\) is rotated about the \(x\)-axis. The volume is

  1. \(24\pi\)
  2. \(6\pi\)
  3. \(12\pi\)
  4. \(36\pi\)
  5. \(16\pi\)

\(\pi\int_{-3}^3 4\left(1 - \frac{x^2}{9}\right)dx = 16\pi\) (\(=\frac43\pi ab^2\)).

7. \(X \sim N(20, 3^2)\) and \(Y \sim N(15, 4^2)\) are independent. The distribution of \(2X - Y\) is

  1. \(N(25, 2^2)\)
  2. \(N(25, 5^2)\)
  3. \(N(25, 52)\)
  4. \(N(25, 20)\)
  5. \(N(5, 52)\)

Mean \(40 - 15 = 25\); variance \(4(9) + 16 = 52\).

8. A random sample of 64 gives \(\bar x = 50\), with \(\sigma = 8\). An approximate 95% confidence interval for \(\mu\) is

  1. \((34.32, 65.68)\)
  2. \((48.04, 51.96)\)
  3. \((49.75, 50.25)\)
  4. \((48.36, 51.64)\)
  5. \((42, 58)\)

\(50 \pm 1.96\times1\). D uses 90%.

9. For \(H_0: \mu = 100\), \(H_1: \mu > 100\), with \(\sigma = 12\), \(n = 36\) and \(\bar x = 103\), the p-value is closest to

  1. 0.134
  2. 0.401
  3. 0.023
  4. 0.067
  5. 0.933

\(z = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5\); \(\Pr(Z > 1.5) \approx 0.067\). A is the two-tailed value.

10. A particle has \(\underset{\sim}{r}(t) = 2\cos t\,\underset{\sim}{i} + 2\sin t\,\underset{\sim}{j} + t\,\underset{\sim}{k}\). Its speed is

  1. \(\sqrt5\)
  2. 3
  3. \(\sqrt3\)
  4. 5
  5. varies with \(t\)

\(|\underset{\sim}{v}| = \sqrt{4\sin^2t + 4\cos^2t + 1} = \sqrt5\).

11. \(\underset{\sim}{i} + 2\underset{\sim}{j} - \underset{\sim}{k}\) and \(2\underset{\sim}{i} + m\underset{\sim}{j} + 4\underset{\sim}{k}\) are perpendicular when \(m =\)

  1. −1
  2. 2
  3. −3
  4. 3
  5. 1

\(2 + 2m - 4 = 0\).

12. A particle moves so that \(v^2 = 16 - x^2\). Its acceleration when \(x = 2\) is

  1. \(2\sqrt3\)
  2. 12
  3. \(-2\)
  4. \(-4\)
  5. 0

\(a = \frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac12v^2\right) = \frac{d}{dx}\left(8 - \frac{x^2}{2}\right) = -x\).

Section B: extended response

Q1 (complex numbers). (a) Find the four solutions of \(z^4 = -16\) in Cartesian form. (b) The solutions form a square on an Argand diagram. Find its area. (c) Hence write \(z^4 + 16\) as a product of two real quadratic factors.

(a) \(-16 = 16\operatorname{cis}\pi\), so \(z = 2\operatorname{cis}\left(\frac\pi4 + \frac{k\pi}{2}\right)\). [1] Solutions \(\pm\sqrt2 \pm \sqrt2 i\). [2]

(b) Side from \(\sqrt2 + \sqrt2 i\) to \(-\sqrt2 + \sqrt2 i\) is \(2\sqrt2\). [1] Area 8. [1]

(c) Pair conjugates: \((z^2 - 2\sqrt2 z + 4)(z^2 + 2\sqrt2 z + 4)\). [2]

Q2 (DEs and kinematics). An object falls from rest with acceleration \(a = 9.8 - 0.2v\) m/s² (down positive). (a) Find its terminal velocity. (b) Find \(v\) in terms of \(t\). (c) Find the distance fallen in the first 5 s. (d) Find when it reaches 95% of terminal velocity.

(a) \(0 = 9.8 - 0.2v \Rightarrow v_T = 49\) m/s. [1]

(b) \(\frac{dv}{dt} = 0.2(49 - v)\); \(\int\frac{dv}{49 - v} = \int 0.2\,dt\). [1] \(49 - v = 49e^{-0.2t}\). [1] \(v = 49(1 - e^{-0.2t})\). [1]

(c) \(\int_0^5 49(1 - e^{-0.2t})\,dt = 49\big[t + 5e^{-0.2t}\big]_0^5\). [1] \(= 245e^{-1} \approx 90.13\) m. [1]

(d) \(e^{-0.2t} = 0.05 \Rightarrow t = 5\log_e 20\). [1] \(\approx 14.98\) s. [1]

Q3 (vectors). Two drones have \(\underset{\sim}{r}_A(t) = (1 + t)\underset{\sim}{i} + 2t\underset{\sim}{j} + (3 - t)\underset{\sim}{k}\) and \(\underset{\sim}{r}_B(t) = (3 - t)\underset{\sim}{i} + (1 + t)\underset{\sim}{j} + 2t\underset{\sim}{k}\), \(t \ge 0\). (a) Show they collide, and find where. (b) Find the angle between their velocities, to 0.1°. (c) Find their distance apart at \(t = 0\).

(a) \(i\): \(1 + t = 3 - t \Rightarrow t = 1\). Check \(j\): \(2 = 2\) ✓, \(k\): \(2 = 2\) ✓. [1] Collide at \((2, 2, 2)\) when \(t = 1\). [1]

(b) \(\underset{\sim}{v}_A = (1, 2, -1)\), \(\underset{\sim}{v}_B = (-1, 1, 2)\); dot \(= -1\), magnitudes \(\sqrt6\). [1] \(\cos\theta = -\frac16\), \(\theta \approx 99.6°\). [1]

(c) \(\underset{\sim}{r}_B(0) - \underset{\sim}{r}_A(0) = (2, 1, -3)\). [1] Distance \(\sqrt{14}\). [1]

Q4 (inference). A café machine fills cups with volume \(N(\mu, 5^2)\) mL; the claim is \(\mu = 250\). A sample of 25 cups has \(\bar x = 247.6\). (a) State hypotheses to test whether the mean differs from the claim. (b) Find the p-value. (c) Conclude at the 5% and 1% levels. (d) Find a 95% CI for \(\mu\). (e) If \(\mu = 250\), find the probability that one cup has more than 5 mL more than another.

(a) \(H_0: \mu = 250\), \(H_1: \mu \ne 250\). [1]

(b) \(z = \frac{-2.4}{1} = -2.4\). [1] \(p = 2\Pr(Z < -2.4) \approx 0.0164\). [1]

(c) Reject \(H_0\) at 5% (evidence the mean differs from 250 mL); do not reject at 1%. [1]

(d) \(247.6 \pm 1.96\): \((245.64, 249.56)\). [1]

(e) \(D = X_1 - X_2 \sim N(0, 50)\). [1] \(\Pr(D > 5) = \Pr(Z > 0.7071)\). [1] \(\approx 0.2398\). [1]
