The cross product

Vector product
\[ \underset{\sim}{a}\times\underset{\sim}{b} = \begin{vmatrix} \underset{\sim}{i} & \underset{\sim}{j} & \underset{\sim}{k} \\ a_1 & a_2 & a_3 \\ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \end{vmatrix} = (a_2b_3 - a_3b_2)\underset{\sim}{i} - (a_1b_3 - a_3b_1)\underset{\sim}{j} + (a_1b_2 - a_2b_1)\underset{\sim}{k} \]

\(\underset{\sim}{a}\times\underset{\sim}{b}\) is perpendicular to both, with magnitude \(|\underset{\sim}{a}||\underset{\sim}{b}|\sin\theta\) = the area of the parallelogram they span. \(\underset{\sim}{b}\times\underset{\sim}{a} = -\underset{\sim}{a}\times\underset{\sim}{b}\).

Find the area of the triangle with vertices \(A(1, 0, 0)\), \(B(0, 2, 0)\) and \(C(0, 0, 3)\).

Area \(= \frac12|\overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC}|\).

\(\overrightarrow{AB} = -\underset{\sim}{i} + 2\underset{\sim}{j}\), \(\overrightarrow{AC} = -\underset{\sim}{i} + 3\underset{\sim}{k}\).

\(\overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC} = (6 - 0)\underset{\sim}{i} - (-3 - 0)\underset{\sim}{j} + (0 + 2)\underset{\sim}{k} = 6\underset{\sim}{i} + 3\underset{\sim}{j} + 2\underset{\sim}{k}\).

\(|\cdot| = \sqrt{36 + 9 + 4} = 7\), so the area is \(\frac72\).

Lines in 3D

Line through A with direction d
\[ \underset{\sim}{r} = \underset{\sim}{a} + t\underset{\sim}{d} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad x = a_1 + td_1,\ y = a_2 + td_2,\ z = a_3 + td_3 \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad \frac{x - a_1}{d_1} = \frac{y - a_2}{d_2} = \frac{z - a_3}{d_3} \]

Do \(\underset{\sim}{r}_1 = (1, 2, 0) + s(1, -1, 2)\) and \(\underset{\sim}{r}_2 = (2, 0, 1) + t(0, 1, 1)\) intersect?

Equate components with different parameters. Solve two equations, test the third. Not parallel and no intersection → skew.

\(x\): \(1 + s = 2 \Rightarrow s = 1\). \(y\): \(2 - s = t \Rightarrow t = 1\).

\(z\): \(2s = 2\) and \(1 + t = 2\) ✓ consistent.

Yes, they meet at \((2, 1, 2)\).

Planes

Plane through A with normal n
\[ \underset{\sim}{r}\cdot\underset{\sim}{n} = \underset{\sim}{a}\cdot\underset{\sim}{n} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad n_1x + n_2y + n_3z = d \]

Given three points, get two vectors in the plane and take their cross product for \(\underset{\sim}{n}\).

Find the Cartesian equation of the plane through \(A(1, 0, 0)\), \(B(0, 2, 0)\) and \(C(0, 0, 3)\).

Reuse the cross product above: \(\underset{\sim}{n} = 6\underset{\sim}{i} + 3\underset{\sim}{j} + 2\underset{\sim}{k}\). Substitute \(A\) to find \(d\).

\(6x + 3y + 2z = d\); at \(A\): \(d = 6\).

\(6x + 3y + 2z = 6\) (check \(B\): 6 ✓, \(C\): 6 ✓; equivalently \(x + \frac y2 + \frac z3 = 1\)).

Find where the line \(\underset{\sim}{r} = (1, 1, 1) + t(1, 2, -1)\) meets the plane \(2x - y + 3z = 9\).

Substitute the parametric coordinates into the plane equation and solve for \(t\).

\(2(1 + t) - (1 + 2t) + 3(1 - t) = 9 \Rightarrow 4 - 3t = 9\), \(t = -\frac53\).

\(\left(-\frac23, -\frac73, \frac83\right)\).

Angles and distance
\[ \text{between planes: } \cos\theta = \frac{|\underset{\sim}{n}_1\cdot\underset{\sim}{n}_2|}{|\underset{\sim}{n}_1||\underset{\sim}{n}_2|} \qquad \text{line and plane: } \sin\theta = \frac{|\underset{\sim}{d}\cdot\underset{\sim}{n}|}{|\underset{\sim}{d}||\underset{\sim}{n}|} \qquad \text{point to plane: } D = \frac{|n_1x_0 + n_2y_0 + n_3z_0 - d|}{|\underset{\sim}{n}|} \]

The angle between a line and a plane uses sine, because the dot product gives the angle with the normal, which is the complement. A line is parallel to a plane when \(\underset{\sim}{d}\cdot\underset{\sim}{n} = 0\) (then it lies in it or never meets it).

Cross products are fine by hand in Exam 1, so practise the determinant expansion with the minus sign on \(\underset{\sim}{j}\). In Exam 2, use CAS crossP and dotP, but still write the set-up.

Summary

  • \(\underset{\sim}{a}\times\underset{\sim}{b}\) ⟂ both; \(|\underset{\sim}{a}\times\underset{\sim}{b}|\) = parallelogram area (half for a triangle).
  • Line: \(\underset{\sim}{r} = \underset{\sim}{a} + t\underset{\sim}{d}\). Plane: \(\underset{\sim}{r}\cdot\underset{\sim}{n} = \underset{\sim}{a}\cdot\underset{\sim}{n}\), i.e. \(n_1x + n_2y + n_3z = d\).
  • Intersections: substitute parametric form. Skew: not parallel and no solution.
  • Line–plane angle uses sine; point–plane distance \(\frac{|n\cdot p - d|}{|n|}\).
