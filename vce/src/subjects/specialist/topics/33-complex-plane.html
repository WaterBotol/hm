Distance is modulus

\(|z - a|\) is the distance between the points \(z\) and \(a\) in the complex plane. Almost every set here is built from that idea.

\(|z + 2 - i|\) is the distance from \(-2 + i\), not from \(2 - i\): write it as \(|z - (-2 + i)|\) first. And a ray \(\operatorname{Arg}(z - a) = \theta\) excludes its endpoint \(a\) (Arg of 0 is undefined): draw an open circle there.

Circles and lines

Find the Cartesian equation of \(\{z : |z - 2| = |z + 4i|\}\) and describe it.

It's the set of points equidistant from \(2\) and \(-4i\): the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining \((2, 0)\) and \((0, -4)\). Substitute \(z = x + yi\), square both sides and simplify.

\((x - 2)^2 + y^2 = x^2 + (y + 4)^2\).

\(-4x + 4 = 8y + 16 \Rightarrow 4x + 8y + 12 = 0\).

\(x + 2y + 3 = 0\), i.e. \(y = -\frac12 x - \frac32\): a line through the midpoint \((1, -2)\), perpendicular to the segment (gradient 2).

Show that \(\{z : |z - 3| = 2|z|\}\) is a circle and find its centre and radius.

When the two distances aren't equal, the set is a circle (an Apollonius circle). Substitute, square, then complete the square.

\((x - 3)^2 + y^2 = 4(x^2 + y^2) \Rightarrow 3x^2 + 3y^2 + 6x - 9 = 0 \Rightarrow x^2 + 2x + y^2 = 3\).

\((x + 1)^2 + y^2 = 4\): centre \(-1\) (the point \((-1, 0)\)), radius 2.

Rays and regions

Find the point where \(\operatorname{Arg}(z) = \frac{\pi}{4}\) meets \(|z - 2| = 2\).

The ray is \(y = x\) with \(x > 0\). Substitute into the circle's Cartesian equation and reject solutions not on the ray.

Circle: \((x - 2)^2 + y^2 = 4\). With \(y = x\): \(2x^2 - 4x = 0 \Rightarrow x = 0\) or \(x = 2\).

\(x = 0\) is the origin, which isn't on the ray. So the intersection is \(z = 2 + 2i\).

For \(z\) on the circle \(|z - (3 + 4i)| = 2\), find the maximum and minimum values of \(|z|\).

\(|z|\) is the distance from the origin. The nearest and farthest points of a circle from a point lie on the line through that point and the centre: distance to the centre ± radius.

Distance from 0 to the centre: \(|3 + 4i| = 5\).

\(\max|z| = 5 + 2 = 7\), \(\min|z| = 5 - 2 = 3\).

Regions: "\(\le\)" or "\(\ge\)" gives a shaded region with a solid boundary; "\(<\)" or "\(>\)" a dashed boundary. \(|z - a| < |z - b|\) is the half-plane on \(a\)'s side of the bisector. \(\frac{\pi}{6} \le \operatorname{Arg}(z) \le \frac{\pi}{3}\) is a wedge from the origin.

Exam 1 questions often combine this with roots: "the roots of \(z^3 = 8i\) lie on which circle?", or "sketch \(\{z : |z| \le 2\} \cap \{z : \operatorname{Re}(z) \ge 1\}\) and find its area". Label key points with exact coordinates.

Practice

The set \(\{z : |z + 1 - 2i| = 3\}\) is a circle with centre

  1. \((1, -2)\)
  2. \((-1, 2)\)
  3. \((1, 2)\)
  4. \((-1, -2)\)

\(|z - (-1 + 2i)| = 3\).

\(\{z : |z - i| = |z + i|\}\) is

  1. the imaginary axis
  2. the circle \(|z| = 1\)
  3. the line \(y = x\)
  4. the real axis

Points equidistant from \(i\) and \(-i\): the perpendicular bisector, the real axis.

The set \(\{z : \operatorname{Arg}(z - 1) = \frac{\pi}{2}\}\) is

  1. the vertical half-line above \(1\), not including \(1\)
  2. the line \(x = 1\)
  3. the vertical half-line above \(1\), including \(1\)
  4. the half-line \(y = x - 1\), \(x > 1\)

A ray from \(1\) at angle \(\frac\pi2\); the endpoint is excluded.

The minimum value of \(|z - 4|\) for \(z\) on the circle \(|z| = 1\) is

  1. 4
  2. 5
  3. 3
  4. 1

Distance from 4 to the centre 0 is 4; minus the radius 1.

Find the Cartesian equation of \(\{z : |z - 1 + i| = |z - 3 - i|\}\).

\((x - 1)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = (x - 3)^2 + (y - 1)^2\). [1]

\(-2x + 2y + 2 = -6x - 2y + 10 \Rightarrow 4x + 4y = 8\), i.e. \(y = 2 - x\). [1]

Find the area of the region \(\{z : |z| \le 2\} \cap \{z : \operatorname{Re}(z) \ge 1\}\).

A circular segment of the disc of radius 2 cut off by \(x = 1\). The chord meets the circle at \(1 \pm \sqrt3 i\), subtending angle \(\frac{2\pi}{3}\) at the centre. [1]

Segment area \(= \frac12 r^2(\theta - \sin\theta) = 2\left(\frac{2\pi}{3} - \frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)\). [1]

\(= \frac{4\pi}{3} - \sqrt3\). [1]

Find the complex numbers satisfying both \(|z| = \sqrt2\) and \(|z - 1| = |z - i|\).

The bisector of \(1\) and \(i\) is \(y = x\). [1]

\(x^2 + x^2 = 2 \Rightarrow x = \pm1\). [1]

\(z = 1 + i\) or \(z = -1 - i\). [1]

Summary

  • \(|z - a|\) is distance from \(a\): circles, discs and bisectors follow.
  • \(\operatorname{Arg}(z - a) = \theta\) is a ray from \(a\), endpoint excluded.
  • Convert with \(z = x + yi\) and square both sides; complete the square for circles.
  • Max/min distance to a circle: distance to centre ± radius.
