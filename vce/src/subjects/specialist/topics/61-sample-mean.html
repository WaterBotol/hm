The sample mean is a random variable

Take a random sample \(X_1, \dots, X_n\) from a population with mean \(\mu\) and sd \(\sigma\). The sample mean \(\bar X = \frac{X_1 + \dots + X_n}{n}\) changes from sample to sample.

Mean and sd of X̄
\[ E(\bar X) = \mu, \qquad \operatorname{sd}(\bar X) = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n} \]

From linear combinations: \(\operatorname{Var}(\bar X) = \frac{1}{n^2}\cdot n\sigma^2 = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\).

For large \(n\) (rule of thumb \(n \ge 30\)), \(\bar X\) is approximately normal, \(\bar X \sim N\left(\mu, \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\right)\), whatever the shape of the population. If the population itself is normal, \(\bar X\) is exactly normal for any \(n\).

Battery lifetimes have mean 40 hours and sd 6 hours (distribution unknown). A random sample of 36 batteries is tested. Find the approximate probability that the sample mean exceeds 42 hours.

\(n = 36\) is large, so by the CLT \(\bar X \approx N\left(40, \left(\frac{6}{6}\right)^2\right) = N(40, 1)\).

\(\operatorname{sd}(\bar X) = \frac{6}{\sqrt{36}} = 1\).

\(\Pr(\bar X > 42) = \Pr(Z > 2) \approx 0.0228\).

A population has \(\sigma = 15\). How large must \(n\) be for \(\operatorname{sd}(\bar X)\) to be at most 2?

Solve \(\frac{15}{\sqrt n} \le 2\) and round up.

\(\sqrt n \ge 7.5 \Rightarrow n \ge 56.25\).

\(n = 57\).

Use \(\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}\) for questions about \(\bar X\), but plain \(\sigma\) for a single observation. "One battery lasts over 42 h" and "the mean of 36 lasts over 42 h" are very different probabilities (≈0.37 vs ≈0.023).

VCAA wants the distribution stated explicitly: "\(\bar X\) is approximately normal with mean 40 and sd 1" plus the reason (CLT, large \(n\), or normal population). Quadrupling \(n\) halves the sd.

Practice

A population has \(\mu = 80\), \(\sigma = 12\). For samples of size 16, \(\operatorname{sd}(\bar X) =\)

  1. 3
  2. 0.75
  3. 12
  4. 9

\(\frac{12}{4} = 3\).

To halve the standard deviation of the sample mean, the sample size must be

  1. halved
  2. doubled
  3. multiplied by 4
  4. squared

\(\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{4n}} = \frac12\cdot\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}\).

A population is strongly right-skewed. For random samples of size 4, the distribution of \(\bar X\) is

  1. exactly normal
  2. approximately normal by the CLT
  3. uniform
  4. likely still skewed; the CLT needs a larger \(n\)

\(n = 4\) is too small for the CLT to rescue a skewed population.

Heights are \(N(170, 8^2)\) cm. The probability that the mean of 4 randomly chosen people is between 166 and 174 cm is closest to

  1. 0.38
  2. 0.68
  3. 0.95
  4. 0.99

\(\operatorname{sd}(\bar X) = 4\): within one sd, ≈ 0.68.

Bags of rice have mass mean 1002 g and sd 4 g. For a random sample of 64 bags, find the approximate probability the sample mean is less than 1001 g.

\(\operatorname{sd}(\bar X) = \frac{4}{8} = 0.5\). [1]

By the CLT, \(\bar X \approx N(1002, 0.5^2)\). [1]

\(\Pr(\bar X < 1001) = \Pr(Z < -2) \approx 0.0228\). [1]

Waiting times have mean 5 min and sd 5 min (exponential-like). For a sample of 100 waits, find \(c\) such that \(\Pr(\bar X > c) = 0.05\).

CLT: \(\bar X \approx N(5, 0.5^2)\). [1]

\(c = 5 + 1.6449(0.5)\). [1]

\(c \approx 5.82\) min. [1]

Summary

  • \(E(\bar X) = \mu\), \(\operatorname{sd}(\bar X) = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}\).
  • CLT: \(\bar X\) approx normal for large \(n\) (≈30+); exactly normal if the population is normal.
  • Bigger \(n\) → narrower distribution; ×4 sample size halves the sd.
