General Mathematics Exam 2 · real exam questions, easier

Every question here is a real VCAA exam question. They were chosen by ranking every question from the 2023–2025 exams by how Victorian students actually scored on it (state average ÷ marks), within each area of study, so the paper keeps the real exam’s balance of about 24 marks of data analysis and 12 each of the other three areas. This paper takes the highest band. The questions themselves are in the official PDFs (VCAA copyright), so open each one with its link. Answer here, then mark yourself against the guide.

How the state went on these exact questions: an average of 40 / 60 (66%). Beat that.

Oyster sizes: a frequency table, a percentage segmented bar chart, and a two-way table comparing two farms

State average for the whole question: 4 / 5 (80%).

Complete the frequency table (numbers) for 20 oysters

  • small 7, medium 10, large 3, total 20

Small 7, medium 10, large 3, total 20. Tally marks or a missing total weren’t accepted.

State average 1 / 1 (100%); 95% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 5% · 1: 95%

Draw a percentage segmented bar chart from the table

  • Segments at 35%, 50% and 15% (boundaries at 35 and 85), matching the key

One bar split at 35% and 85%. Well done.

State average 0.8 / 1 (80%); 84% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 16% · 1: 84%

Percentage of all oysters graded large, to the nearest whole number

  • 17%

(44 + 46) ÷ 530 ≈ 17%.

State average 0.9 / 1 (90%); 87% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 13% · 1: 87%

Does the two-way table support farm A growing larger oysters? Compare two percentages

  • Large at farm A 21% and farm B 14%
  • Yes, because farm A’s percentage of large oysters is higher

Yes: 44 ÷ 210 ≈ 21% of farm A’s oysters are large vs 46 ÷ 320 ≈ 14% for farm B. You had to state the comparison explicitly.

State average 1.3 / 2 (65%); 50% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 19% · 1: 32% · 2: 50%

Monthly ice cream consumption time series over three years: seasonality, deseasonalising and a seasonal index

State average for the whole question: 1.7 / 4 (42%).

A feature of the plot that shows seasonality

Mark distribution: 0: 74% · 1: 26%

Deseasonalised April 2010 value given a seasonal index of 1.05

  • 0.17 L/person

Actual April value ÷ 1.05 ≈ 0.17.

State average 0.7 / 1 (70%); 72% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 28% · 1: 72%

Show that the July 2011 seasonal index is 1.10 from a year of monthly data

  • Monthly mean for 2011 ≈ 0.167
  • Index = 0.183 ÷ 0.16700 ≈ 1.0958 ≈ 1.10

Mean of the 12 months ≈ 0.167; 0.183 ÷ 0.167 ≈ 1.096 = 1.10. Both calculations were needed.

How Victoria went on this paper

These statistics are the real published results for each question in the year it was set, compiled from the VCAA examiners’ reports. A student scoring at the state average on every question would get 40 / 60 (66%) on this paper.

  • 2023 Exam 2 Q5 (A $30 000 motorcycle loan at 6.4% p.a. compounding quarterly, repaid over six years): 53% of available marks

What most students got right
