Median and quartiles

Put the data in order. The median splits it in half: it is the \(\frac{n + 1}{2}\)th value. The quartiles split each half again.

  • Q1 (lower quartile): the median of the lower half.
  • Q3 (upper quartile): the median of the upper half.
  • If \(n\) is odd, leave the median itself out of both halves. This is the method VCAA and your CAS use.
Spread
\[ \text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1 \qquad \text{range} = \text{max} - \text{min} \]

The IQR is the spread of the middle 50% of the data.

The five-number summary is min, Q1, median, Q3, max.

Boxplots and outliers

A boxplot draws the five-number summary: the box runs from Q1 to Q3 with a line at the median, and whiskers run out to the smallest and largest values that are not outliers. Possible outliers are plotted as separate points.

Fences (on the formula sheet)
\[ \text{lower fence} = Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR} \qquad \text{upper fence} = Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR} \]

A value below the lower fence or above the upper fence is a possible outlier. A value exactly on a fence is not an outlier.

Each of the four sections of a boxplot (lower whisker, lower box, upper box, upper whisker) holds about 25% of the data. So about 50% of values are above the median, 75% are above Q1, and 25% are above Q3. A longer section means the same 25% is more spread out, not that it holds more values.

When there is an outlier, the whisker ends at the largest non-outlier value, so the maximum is the outlier point. Read the max from the dot, not the whisker.

For the data 3, 5, 6, 8, 9, 11, 12, 14, 15, 18, 32, find the five-number summary and show that 32 is an outlier.

With 11 values the median is the 6th value; leave it out of both halves to find the quartiles. Then compare the extreme values with the fences.

Median = 6th value = 11. Lower half 3, 5, 6, 8, 9 gives Q1 = 6; upper half 12, 14, 15, 18, 32 gives Q3 = 15.

IQR = 15 − 6 = 9. Upper fence = 15 + 1.5 × 9 = 28.5; lower fence = 6 − 13.5 = −7.5.

Five-number summary: 3, 6, 11, 15, 32. Since 32 > 28.5, 32 is a possible outlier; no value is below −7.5.

Mean and standard deviation

The mean \(\bar x\) is the sum divided by the number of values. The standard deviation \(s\) measures the typical distance of values from the mean. Find both with your CAS (one-variable statistics); you don't calculate \(s\) by hand in the exam.

Mean and standard deviation
\[ \bar x = \frac{\sum x}{n} \qquad s = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x - \bar x)^2}{n - 1}} \]
Shape of the dataCentreSpreadWhy
Symmetric, no outliersmean (or median)standard deviation (or IQR)All give similar values
Skewed, or has outliersmedianIQRThe median and IQR are resistant: extreme values barely move them

For the data above, the mean is 12.1 and \(s \approx 8.0\). Remove the outlier 32 and the mean drops to 10.1 and \(s\) to 4.8, but the median only moves from 11 to 10. The mean is pulled towards the tail, so in a positively skewed distribution the mean is greater than the median.

Comparing distributions

To compare two or more groups, compare centre and spread with numbers, in context.

“The median travel time for bus users (35 min) is higher than for car users (22 min). Bus times are also more variable: the IQR is 18 min compared with 9 min.” Quote the actual values: “higher” on its own usually gets no marks.

A boxplot of the ages of 200 club members has Q1 = 24, median = 31, Q3 = 42 and max = 70. About how many members are aged over 24, and what percentage are aged between 31 and 42?

Each section of a boxplot holds about 25% of the data.

Over Q1: about 75% of 200 = 150 members.

About 150 members are over 24, and about 25% are aged between the median (31) and Q3 (42).

  4. 12

The median and IQR are not affected by the long tail or outliers.

On a boxplot the upper whisker is twice as long as the lower whisker. This means

  1. the top 25% of values are more spread out than the bottom 25%
  2. twice as many values lie in the upper whisker
  3. the mean is above Q3
  4. there must be an outlier

Each whisker holds about 25% of the values; a longer one means those values are more spread out.

The five-number summary of a data set is 4, 10, 13, 16, 26. The number of possible outliers is

  1. 0, because 26 is the maximum
  2. 1, the value 4
  3. 1, the value 26
  4. 2

IQR = 6. Fences: 10 − 9 = 1 and 16 + 9 = 25. Since 26 > 25 it is an outlier; 4 > 1 is not.

The numbers of goals scored in 12 games were: 0, 1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 5, 6, 11. Find the five-number summary and determine whether 11 is an outlier.

Median = average of the 6th and 7th values = \(\frac{2 + 3}{2} = 2.5\). [1]

Lower half 0, 1, 1, 2, 2, 2: Q1 = 1.5; upper half 3, 3, 4, 5, 6, 11: Q3 = 4.5. Summary: 0, 1.5, 2.5, 4.5, 11. [1]

IQR = 3, upper fence = 4.5 + 4.5 = 9. Since 11 > 9, it is a possible outlier. [1]

Summary

  • Q1 and Q3 are the medians of the lower and upper halves, leaving out the median when \(n\) is odd. IQR = Q3 − Q1, the spread of the middle 50%.
  • Outliers are below \(Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR}\) or above \(Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}\) (on a fence is not an outlier). Whiskers end at the most extreme non-outliers.
  • About 25% in each of the four sections.
  • For skewed data or data with outliers: the median and IQR are resistant; the mean is dragged towards the tail.
