The recurrence relation

An annuity investment grows two ways: interest on the balance, and a regular deposit added every period. Both push the balance up.

Annuity investment
\[ V_0 = \text{initial deposit}, \qquad V_{n+1} = R\,V_n + d \]

\(d\) is the regular deposit. In Finance Solver: PV negative (you deposit it), Pmt negative (you keep depositing), FV positive (it's yours at the end).

Using Finance Solver

Kim invests $5000 at 4.8% per annum compounding monthly and adds $200 at the end of every month. Find (a) the value after 10 years and (b) the total interest earned.

Solve for FV; total interest = final value − everything deposited.

(a) N = 120, I% = 4.8, PV = −5000, Pmt = −200, PpY = CpY = 12: FV = 38 799.03.

(b) Deposits: \(5000 + 120 \times 200 = 29\,000\). Interest: \(38\,799.03 - 29\,000\).

(a) $38 799.03. (b) $9799.03.

How many months will it take Kim's investment to first exceed $50 000?

Solve for N with FV = 50 000, then round up (the balance only grows at the end of each month).

I% = 4.8, PV = −5000, Pmt = −200, FV = 50 000: N = 149.8.

150 months (12.5 years).

(a) Starting from $0, what monthly deposit at 4.8% per annum compounding monthly reaches $100 000 in 15 years? (b) What annual interest rate would grow $10 000 plus $300 per month to $60 000 in 10 years (monthly compounding)?

Solve for Pmt in (a) and for I(%) in (b).

(a) N = 180, I% = 4.8, PV = 0, FV = 100 000: Pmt = −380.41.

(b) N = 120, PV = −10 000, Pmt = −300, FV = 60 000: I% = 4.24.

(a) $380.41 per month. (b) About 4.24% per annum.

All four use \(V_{n+1} = R\,V_n + d\): compound interest (\(d = 0\)); reducing balance loan and annuity (\(d < 0\), payments out); annuity investment (\(d > 0\), deposits in). Depreciation uses \(R < 1\) (reducing balance) or \(R = 1\) (flat rate, unit cost).

“\(V_0 = 2000\), \(V_{n+1} = 1.015V_n + 500\), where \(n\) is the number of quarters” describes an investment of $2000 earning 6% per annum compounding quarterly (1.5% per quarter), with $500 deposited at the end of each quarter.

Practice

$2000 is invested at 6% per annum compounding quarterly, with $500 added each quarter. The recurrence relation is

  1. \(V_{n+1} = 1.015V_n + 500\)
  2. \(V_{n+1} = 1.06V_n + 500\)
  3. \(V_{n+1} = 1.015V_n - 500\)
  4. \(V_{n+1} = 1.5V_n + 500\)

\(\frac{6}{4} = 1.5\%\) per quarter; deposits are added.

The value of that investment after 5 years is closest to

  1. $12 500
  2. $12 000
  3. $14 256
  4. $16 932

N = 20, I% = 6, PV = −2000, Pmt = −500, PpY = CpY = 4: FV ≈ 14 255.54.

For a savings plan, the Finance Solver signs should be

  1. PV +, Pmt +, FV −
  2. PV −, Pmt −, FV +
  3. PV −, Pmt +, FV +
  4. PV +, Pmt −, FV 0

You pay in at the start and each period (−), and get the money back at the end (+).

Ava deposits $300 at the end of each month (no initial deposit) at 5.4% per annum compounding monthly. After 12 years her balance is closest to

  1. $43 200
  2. $51 840
  3. $56 100
  4. $60 596

N = 144, I% = 5.4, PV = 0, Pmt = −300: FV ≈ 60 595.78.

Ava's 12-year balance ($60 595.78) is then left to grow with no more deposits at 5.4% per annum compounding monthly. (a) Write a recurrence relation for the balance \(n\) months after deposits stop. (b) Find the balance 3 years later. (c) How much interest was earned over all 15 years?

(a) \(V_0 = 60\,595.78\), \(V_{n+1} = 1.0045V_n\). [1]

(b) \(1.0045^{36} \times 60\,595.78 \approx \$71\,226.28\). [1]

(c) Deposits: \(144 \times 300 = 43\,200\); interest \(= 71\,226.28 - 43\,200 = \$28\,026.28\). [1]

Summary

  • \(V_{n+1} = RV_n + d\). PV −, Pmt −, FV +.
  • Interest = periodic rate × previous balance; new balance = previous balance + interest + deposit.
  • Solve for N and round up. Total interest = final value − all deposits (including the initial one).
  • Compound interest: \(d = 0\); loan and annuity: \(d < 0\); annuity investment: \(d > 0\).
