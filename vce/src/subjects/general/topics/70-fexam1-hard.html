General Mathematics Exam 1 · Further Maths questions, harder

These are real VCAA questions from the 2020–2022 Further Mathematics exams, the subject General Maths replaced in 2023. Only the core (data analysis, recursion and finance) and the Matrices and Networks modules are used, and questions on content that isn’t in the current General Maths study design (solving simultaneous equations with matrices) are left out. They were ranked by how Further Maths students actually scored on them (percentage correct) within each area of study, and this paper takes the lowest band, with 16 data analysis, 8 recursion and finance, 8 matrices and 8 networks questions. The questions themselves are in the official PDFs (VCAA copyright), so open each one with its link. Answer here, then mark yourself against the guide.

How Further Maths students went on these exact questions: an average of 15 / 40 (38%). Beat that.

Data analysis

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 4 Open in the exam PDF (p. 5) ↗

Which value could be the third quartile of 252 forearm circumferences shown in a histogram

A quarter of 252 is 63. Counting down from the top, the bars above 30.5 cm hold fewer than 63 men and adding the 30.0–30.5 cm bar passes 63, so Q3 lies in that class: 30.3.

50% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 7% · B 13% · C 50% · D 24% · E 5%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 13 Open in the exam PDF (p. 9) ↗

What is always true of a least squares line

It minimises the sum of the squared vertical distances (residuals) from the points to the line. Only a third of students knew this.

34% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 11% · B 23% · C 21% · D 10% · E 34%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 15 Open in the exam PDF (p. 10) ↗

How March rainfall (seasonal index 0.741) must change to be deseasonalised

Deseasonalised = actual ÷ 0.741 = 1.35 × actual: increase by 35%. A and B used 1 − 0.741.

43% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 17% · B 24% · C 9% · D 43% · E 6%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 19 Open in the exam PDF (p. 12) ↗

Median monthly airline passengers in 1960, from a time series plot

Put the 12 monthly values in order: the 6th and 7th are both about 461 000, so that is the median. D came from using the middle months of the year instead of the middle values.

45% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 45% · B 7% · C 9% · D 32% · E 6%.

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 3 Open in the exam PDF (p. 6) ↗

Which two-way frequency table could match the segmented bar chart

Work out column percentages: in A, 91 ÷ 140 = 65% and 90 ÷ 200 = 45% domestic, matching the chart. C reads the percentages straight in as counts with the rows swapped.

50% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 50% · B 13% · C 28% · D 1% · E 8%.

Best display for resting pulse rate against age group

Number of 800 auditionees who scored below 76.0 (mean 69.5, SD 6.5)

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 11 Open in the exam PDF (p. 10) ↗

How many of 10 adults have a predicted weight above their actual weight

Fit weight against height (height is explanatory) and look at the residuals: six are negative, so the prediction is too high six times.

40% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 14% · B 29% · C 11% · D 40% · E 5%.

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 12 Open in the exam PDF (p. 11) ↗

Best description of a quarterly sales time series over 11 years

Sales drift down over time with irregular ups and downs, but the peaks don’t recur at the same quarter each year: a decreasing trend with irregular fluctuations. More students wrongly saw seasonality (E) than chose the right answer.

34% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 4% · B 8% · C 13% · D 34% · E 41%.

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 14 Open in the exam PDF (p. 12) ↗

Three-mean smoothed value for Thursday, given a five-mean value and some daily figures

Five-mean for Thursday = 206, so Tuesday to Saturday total 1030. Take off Tuesday (186) and Saturday (346): Wednesday to Friday total 498, so the three-mean is 498 ÷ 3 = 166.

48% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 8% · B 48% · C 15% · D 25% · E 3%.

Shape of a histogram of possum skull widths

Recursion and financial modelling

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 26 Open in the exam PDF (p. 14) ↗

Rule for the balance after n years at 3% p.a. compounding quarterly

3% p.a. is 0.75% per quarter and there are 4n quarters in n years: Rₙ = 5000 × 1.0075⁴ⁿ. D counts quarters, not years.

20% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 5% · B 18% · C 6% · D 51% · E 20%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 30 Open in the exam PDF (p. 15) ↗

Original sum invested, given two interest rates over 20 years and a $10 000 top-up halfway

Work back: 686 904.09 ÷ (1 + 0.028/12)¹²⁰ − 10 000, then ÷ (1 + 0.032/12)¹²⁰ ≈ $370 000. Finance Solver in two steps does the same.

41% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 10% · B 41% · C 18% · D 15% · E 15%.

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 21 Open in the exam PDF (p. 15) ↗

Effective annual rate, given $3000 grows to $3728.92 in four years compounding monthly

Monthly growth factor = (3728.92 ÷ 3000)^(1/48); the effective rate is that factor to the power 12, minus 1 ≈ 5.59%. A is the nominal rate.

40% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 22% · B 12% · C 13% · D 40% · E 12%.

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 23 Open in the exam PDF (p. 15) ↗

Value of R in a monthly loan recurrence relation, given the loan is repaid in 20 years

Finance Solver with N = 240, PV = 450 000, PMT = −2633, FV = 0 gives I% ≈ 3.6% p.a., so R = 1 + 0.036/12 = 1.003. D forgot to divide by 12.

31% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 31% · B 15% · C 11% · D 33% · E 9%.

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 24 Open in the exam PDF (p. 16) ↗

New interest rate that lets an interest-only start still repay the loan in 20 years

The scheduled repayment for 20 years at 3.14% is $2246.53. Interest-only payments keep the balance at $400 000, so solve for I% with N = 216, PV = 400 000 and PMT = −2246.53: about 2.21%.

33% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 9% · B 33% · C 25% · D 15% · E 16%.

R₂ from R₀ = 2, Rₙ₊₁ = 2 − Rₙ

R₁ = 2 − 2 = 0, then R₂ = 2 − 0 = 2.

2022 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 21 Open in the exam PDF (p. 13) ↗

How many of four statements about nominal and effective rates are true

True: effective equals nominal for annual compounding; it rises with more compounding periods; 12% p.a. nominal is 1% a month. False: an effective rate can’t be lower than the nominal rate. Three.

Matrices

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Matrices module · Question 7 Open in the exam PDF (p. 22) ↗

Percentage buying at Giorgio’s on Friday, from Monday’s split and a transition matrix

Monday to Friday is four steps: S₄ = T⁴S₀ with S₀ = (0.4, 0.6) gives Giorgio’s ≈ 31.9%, about 32%. A (31%) and C (34%) used the wrong number of steps.

38% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 16% · B 38% · C 20% · D 13% · E 12%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Matrices module · Question 8 Open in the exam PDF (p. 22) ↗

Order of the product Cᵀ × (Aᵀ × B)ᵀ from the orders of A, B and C

Aᵀ × B is 4 × 2 times 2 × 3 = 4 × 3, so its transpose is 3 × 4. Cᵀ is 4 × 3, and 4 × 3 times 3 × 4 is 4 × 4.

57% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 5% · B 9% · C 14% · D 14% · E 57%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Matrices module · Question 10 Open in the exam PDF (p. 23) ↗

Which statement about the unknown entries of a transition matrix is true, given S₀ and S₁

Columns add to 1: j = 0.4, m = 0.5 and l + n = 0.7. From S₁, 20m + 18 = 28 confirms m = 0.5 and 16 + 40n = 20 gives n = 0.1, so l = 0.6 = m + n.

41% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 19% · B 12% · C 11% · D 16% · E 41%.

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Matrices module · Question 5 Open in the exam PDF (p. 20) ↗

Which matrix expression is defined, given A is 7 × 7 and B is 10 × 7

Bᵀ is 7 × 10, so A(Bᵀ) is 7 × 7 times 7 × 10: defined. AB and AB² don’t match up, BA isn’t square so it has no inverse, and A² − BA subtracts matrices of different orders.

45% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 9% · B 15% · C 13% · D 17% · E 45%.

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Matrices module · Question 7 Open in the exam PDF (p. 21) ↗

S₂ from Sₙ₊₁ = TSₙ − C, given T, S₀ and S₁

Find C first: C = TS₀ − S₁ = (3, −1, 2). Then S₂ = TS₁ − C = (23.04, 55.78, 16.18).

40% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 40% · B 12% · C 27% · D 14% · E 6%.

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Matrices module · Question 8 Open in the exam PDF (p. 21) ↗

Percentage of marsupials expected to stay at the same feeding station from Tuesday to Wednesday

Tuesday’s state is TS₀ = (25, 35, 40). Stayers = 0.4 × 25 + 0.5 × 35 + 0.6 × 40 = 51.5%. C used Monday’s state instead.

29% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 13% · B 17% · C 27% · D 29% · E 11%.

2022 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Matrices module · Question 7 Open in the exam PDF (p. 19) ↗

Where element m₃₁ goes when a column matrix is multiplied by a permutation matrix

Row 1 of K has its 1 in column 3, so p₁₁ = m₃₁. C (no movement) was a common guess.

42% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 42% · B 12% · C 25% · D 14% · E 7%.

2022 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Matrices module · Question 8 Open in the exam PDF (p. 20) ↗

Matrix calculation for each person’s total time to service every department’s computers

Q (4 × 2) times Nᵀ (2 × 3) gives each department’s time for each person. Pre-multiplying by a 1 × 4 row of ones adds the departments: [1 1 1 1] × Q × Nᵀ. B adds across the people instead.

31% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 31% · B 29% · C 7% · D 16% · E 16%.

Networks and decision mathematics

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 7 Open in the exam PDF (p. 27) ↗

How many of four statements hold for a graph of people and the ice-cream flavours they chose

People join only flavours (bipartite). The 8 vertices and 7 edges link up without a cycle, so it is connected, a tree and planar. All four.

11% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 4% · B 31% · C 32% · D 21% · E 11%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 10 Open in the exam PDF (p. 30) ↗

Effect of moving H’s workers to F (F drops to 3 weeks, but F and H can’t run together)

Now: A–C–E–F–G–I = 25 weeks. If F runs weeks 10–13 and H follows (13–19), G ends at 17 and I starts at 19: 24 weeks, one less. The saving can’t be the full three weeks because H now has to wait.

24% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 24% · B 36% · C 12% · D 20% · E 7%.

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 3 Open in the exam PDF (p. 23) ↗

Number of faces of a graph drawn with crossing edges

The graph is four vertices all joined to each other, which can be redrawn without crossings. Then Euler’s formula gives 4 − 6 + f = 2, so f = 4. Most students counted the regions of the crossed drawing (5).

34% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 1% · B 2% · C 34% · D 61% · E 1%.

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 5 Open in the exam PDF (p. 24) ↗

How many of five statements (planar, cycle, bridge, Eulerian trail, Hamiltonian path) hold for a graph

It is planar, has a cycle (a triangle), a bridge (the edge to the degree-1 vertex) and a Hamiltonian path. It has four odd vertices, so no Eulerian trail: four statements.

17% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 8% · B 31% · C 42% · D 17% · E 1%.

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 7 Open in the exam PDF (p. 25) ↗

Number of zeros in the adjacency matrix of a network with a loop and multiple paths

A zero means no direct connection. J–M, K–N and M–N are missing (two zeros each), and J, L, M and N have no loops (four diagonal zeros): 10. K has a loop, so its diagonal entry isn’t zero.

33% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 9% · B 10% · C 33% · D 23% · E 24%.

2022 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 3 Open in the exam PDF (p. 22) ↗

Allocation of four athletes to four events that maximises the total distance

Total each option: A 59.6, B 61.6, C 60.4, D 61.8, E 60.8 m. D is the largest.

39% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 8% · B 27% · C 18% · D 39% · E 6%.

2022 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 4 Open in the exam PDF (p. 23) ↗

Number of edges to remove so a graph with crossings becomes planar

None: the graph can be redrawn with no crossing edges, so it is already planar. Most students counted crossings instead.

21% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 21% · B 22% · C 37% · D 18% · E 2%.

2022 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 8 Open in the exam PDF (p. 24) ↗

Sum of all float times for the same project

The project takes 28 days with critical path B–E–G–H–I–K. The floats are A 4, C 4, D 5, F 3 and J 4, a total of 20.
