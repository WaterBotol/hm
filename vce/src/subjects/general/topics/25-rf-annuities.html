Annuities

An annuity is a lump sum invested to pay you a regular income (for example, retirement savings). Interest is added each period and your payment is taken out, so the balance runs down. Mathematically it is the same as a reducing balance loan, seen from the other side.

Annuity
\[ V_0 = \text{amount invested}, \qquad V_{n+1} = R\,V_n - d \]

\(d\) is the regular payment received. In Finance Solver: PV negative (you invest it), Pmt positive (you receive it), FV = 0 when it runs out.

Pat invests \$400 000 in an annuity at 5.4% per annum compounding monthly and withdraws \$3000 per month. (a) How many withdrawals of \$3000 can be made? (b) What is the balance after 5 years?

Solve for N with FV = 0; then solve for FV with N = 60.

(a) I% = 5.4, PV = −400 000, Pmt = 3000, FV = 0, PpY = CpY = 12: N = 204.08. So 204 full withdrawals (a small final amount, about \$234, is left for a 205th).

(b) N = 60: FV = 317 554.33.

(a) 204 withdrawals of \$3000 (17 years). (b) \$317 554.33.

How much must be invested at 5% per annum compounding monthly to provide an income of \$2500 per month for 20 years, after which nothing is left?

Solve for PV, with N = 240 and FV = 0.

N = 240, I% = 5, Pmt = 2500, FV = 0, PpY = CpY = 12: PV = −378 813.28.

\$378 813.28.

Annuity: PV is negative (money leaves you) and Pmt is positive (money comes to you). This is the opposite of a loan. If Finance Solver returns an error or a nonsense value, check these signs first.

Perpetuities

A perpetuity is an annuity that pays out only the interest each period. The balance never falls, so it pays forever (for example, a scholarship fund).

Perpetuity
\[ d = \frac{r}{100k} \times V_0 \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad V_0 = \frac{d}{\,r/(100k)\,} \qquad V_{n+1} = R\,V_n - d = V_n \]

In Finance Solver: PV = −\(V_0\), FV = +\(V_0\) (same size, opposite sign).

(a) \$500 000 is invested in a perpetuity at 4.2% per annum, paid monthly. Find the monthly payment. (b) How much must be invested at 4.8% per annum (paid monthly) for a perpetuity of \$2000 per month?

The payment is exactly one period's interest.

(a) \(\frac{4.2}{1200} \times 500\,000 = 1750\).

(b) Monthly rate \(\frac{4.8}{1200} = 0.004\): \(V_0 = \frac{2000}{0.004} = 500\,000\).

(a) \$1750 per month. (b) \$500 000.

Payment greater than the interest: the balance falls, so it's an annuity that runs out. Payment equal to the interest: a perpetuity. Payment less than the interest: the balance grows.

Practice

An annuity of \$200 000 earns 6% per annum compounding monthly and pays \$1500 per month. The recurrence relation is

  1. \(V_{n+1} = 1.06V_n - 1500\)
  2. \(V_{n+1} = 1.005V_n - 1500\)
  3. \(V_{n+1} = 1.005V_n + 1500\)
  4. \(V_{n+1} = 0.995V_n\)

0.5% per month, and the payment comes out of the balance.

A perpetuity of \$750 000 earns 5.2% per annum and pays quarterly. Each payment is

  1. \$39 000
  2. \$3250
  3. \$13 000
  4. \$9750

\(\frac{5.2}{400} \times 750\,000 = 9750\).

A school wants a scholarship of \$6000 paid at the end of every year forever. The fund earns 3.75% per annum compounding annually. How much must be invested, and what type of annuity is this?

\(V_0 = \frac{6000}{0.0375} = \$160\,000\). [1]

A perpetuity: only the interest is paid out, so the fund lasts indefinitely. [1]

Summary

  • \(V_{n+1} = RV_n - d\). PV negative, Pmt positive, FV 0 when it runs out.
  • Interest earned = periodic rate × balance; principal reduction = payment − interest; new balance = balance − principal reduction.
  • An annuity paying only the interest, so it lasts forever: \(d = \frac{r}{100k}V_0\), \(V_0 = d \div \frac{r}{100k}\).
  • Payment > interest: runs out. Payment = interest: perpetuity. Payment < interest: grows.
