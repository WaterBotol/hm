General Mathematics Exam 1 · Further Maths questions, medium

These are real VCAA questions from the 2020–2022 Further Mathematics exams, the subject General Maths replaced in 2023. Only the core (data analysis, recursion and finance) and the Matrices and Networks modules are used, and questions on content that isn’t in the current General Maths study design (solving simultaneous equations with matrices) are left out. They were ranked by how Further Maths students actually scored on them (percentage correct) within each area of study, and this paper takes the middle band, with 16 data analysis, 8 recursion and finance, 8 matrices and 8 networks questions. The questions themselves are in the official PDFs (VCAA copyright), so open each one with its link. Answer here, then mark yourself against the guide.

How Further Maths students went on these exact questions: an average of 24 / 40 (60%). Beat that.

Data analysis

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 3 Open in the exam PDF (p. 5) ↗

Shape of the same distribution from its five-number summary

Q3 − median (150) is much bigger than median − Q1 (80) and the maximum is far out, so it is positively skewed. Upper fence = 300 + 1.5 × 230 = 645, and the maximum of 1380 is beyond it: positively skewed with outliers. E picked the wrong direction.

59% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 2% · B 7% · C 59% · D 5% · E 27%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 7 Open in the exam PDF (p. 6) ↗

How many of five insect variables are discrete numerical

Only the number of wings is counted. Colour and species are categorical; length and weight are measured (continuous).

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 16 Open in the exam PDF (p. 10) ↗

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 9 Open in the exam PDF (p. 9) ↗

Mean and SD of heights from two given percentages

16% above 160 means 160 = μ + σ; 2.5% below 115 means 115 = μ − 2σ. Subtracting: 3σ = 45, so σ = 15 and μ = 145.

71% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 8% · B 10% · C 71% · D 7% · E 4%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 28 Open in the exam PDF (p. 15) ↗

Which effective interest rate for a nominal 8% p.a. is wrong

Fortnightly: (1 + 0.08/26)²⁶ − 1 ≈ 8.32%, not 8.31%. The other four are correct.

Matrices

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Matrices module · Question 3 Open in the exam PDF (p. 18) ↗

Smallest power n of a permutation matrix that returns a column of letters to its original order

P moves four of the letters around a cycle and fixes one, so P⁴ is the identity: n = 4. A (n = 1) didn’t check the result.

64% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 19% · B 7% · C 4% · D 64% · E 5%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Matrices module · Question 9 Open in the exam PDF (p. 23) ↗

Which player is sure to rank first on one- and two-step dominances by winning their remaining match

Fill in each possible result and add the one-step and two-step dominances. If Brie beats Andy she scores 8 or 9, ahead of everyone else whichever way the other match goes. No one else can guarantee first place.

Networks and decision mathematics

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 4 Open in the exam PDF (p. 26) ↗

Number of vertices that can be reached from S in a directed graph of one-way streets

Follow the arrows: S reaches T and V directly and then W through V. The edges at U and X point the wrong way, so three vertices. The popular wrong answer (2) missed W.

39% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 1% · B 58% · C 39% · D 2% · E 0%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 5 Open in the exam PDF (p. 26) ↗

Smallest value of an unknown edge x, given the minimum spanning tree length

Prim’s algorithm without x gives 5 + 6 + 7 + 9 + 6 + 5 + 8 + 7 = 53, using the 9 m edge from G to I. If x were less than 9 it would replace that edge and the total would drop, so x ≥ 9.

56% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 13% · B 13% · C 12% · D 56% · E 6%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 6 Open in the exam PDF (p. 27) ↗

Minimum completion time of an activity network

Forward scan: the longest path is B–E–I–J–M = 7 + 4 + 3 + 3 + 5 = 22 days.

61% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 16% · B 6% · C 11% · D 5% · E 61%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 9 Open in the exam PDF (p. 29) ↗

How many of five labelled cuts have capacity equal to the maximum flow

Find each cut’s capacity, counting only edges that cross from the source side to the sink side. The smallest capacity is the maximum flow, and three of the cuts share that value.

60% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 8% · B 18% · C 60% · D 9% · E 4%.

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 6 Open in the exam PDF (p. 25) ↗

Largest duration x for activity E that keeps an 18-hour completion time

Two paths run through E: B–E–J allows x ≤ 6, but C–G–F–E–J (4 + 3 + 1 + x + 7 ≤ 18) allows only x ≤ 3. So x is at most 3. E (6) checked only one path.

46% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 7% · B 46% · C 6% · D 8% · E 31%.

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 8 Open in the exam PDF (p. 26) ↗

Minimum length of road to clear, for different values of two unknown edges

Run Prim’s algorithm for each option. With x = 50 and y = 60 the minimum spanning tree is 361 km. The other pairs give 356, 358 or 363 km, not the totals stated.

48% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 16% · B 48% · C 13% · D 16% · E 6%.

2022 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 5 Open in the exam PDF (p. 23) ↗

Which statement about a connected graph with five vertices and four edges is false

A connected graph with one fewer edge than vertices is a tree, so it can’t contain a cycle. It is still planar, bipartite and contains paths.

54% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 9% · B 8% · C 21% · D 6% · E 54%.

2022 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 6 Open in the exam PDF (p. 23) ↗

Earliest start time of activity G in a network with a dummy activity

G needs both D (6 + 5 = 11) and, through the dummy, B and E (5 + 7 = 12). The later of these is 12.

48% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 20% · B 25% · C 48% · D 4% · E 2%.
