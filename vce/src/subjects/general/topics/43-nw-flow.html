Flow networks

A flow network is a directed network where each weight is a capacity: the most that can flow along that edge (litres per minute, cars per hour, and so on). Flow starts at the source and ends at the sink.

At every vertex other than the source and sink, the total flow in equals the total flow out. Flow along an edge can be anything from 0 up to its capacity, and only in the arrow's direction.

Cuts

A cut is a line that splits the network into two parts, with the source on one side and the sink on the other, so that no flow could get from S to T without crossing it. Its capacity is the total capacity of the edges it crosses from the source side to the sink side.

Find the capacities of Cut 1 and Cut 2 in the figure.

For each edge the cut crosses, ask: does this edge point from the S side to the T side? Only those count.

Cut 1 crosses A → C (4), A → T (5) and B → C (6), all from the S side to the T side: \(4 + 5 + 6 = 15\).

Cut 2 has S and B on one side. It crosses S → A (10), A → B (3) and B → C (6). But A → B points from the T side back to the S side, so it is not counted: \(10 + 6 = 16\).

Cut 1: 15. Cut 2: 16.

The most common error in flow questions is adding an edge that crosses the cut the wrong way. Shade or label the source side, then check each crossed arrow's direction.

Maximum flow = minimum cut

Maximum-flow minimum-cut theorem
\[ \text{maximum flow from source to sink} = \text{capacity of the minimum cut} \]

The minimum cut is the bottleneck. To find the maximum flow, find the cut with the smallest capacity (and check that a flow that big is possible).

Find the maximum flow from S to T and show a flow that achieves it.

Push flow along routes until something is full, then confirm with a cut of the same capacity.

S → A → T: 5 (A → T full). S → A → C → T: 4 (A → C full). S → B → C → T: 6 (B → C full). Total 15.

Cut 1 has capacity 15, and no flow can be bigger than any cut, so 15 is the maximum.

Maximum flow = 15 ML per day. The minimum cut is Cut 1.

To increase the maximum flow, you must increase the capacity of an edge in every minimum cut. Then recheck: another cut may become the new bottleneck. Here, adding 5 to B → C raises the maximum to 17, where cut {S} (\(10 + 7\)) takes over.

Summary

  • Flow starts at the source and ends at the sink; capacity is the most that can flow along an edge.
  • Add the capacities of edges crossing from the source side to the sink side; edges crossing back count 0.
  • The maximum flow from source to sink equals the capacity of the minimum cut.
  • Increase capacity on an edge in every minimum cut, then recheck: another cut may become the bottleneck.
