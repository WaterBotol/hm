General Mathematics Exam 1 · Further Maths questions, easier

These are real VCAA questions from the 2020–2022 Further Mathematics exams, the subject General Maths replaced in 2023. Only the core (data analysis, recursion and finance) and the Matrices and Networks modules are used, and questions on content that isn’t in the current General Maths study design (solving simultaneous equations with matrices) are left out. They were ranked by how Further Maths students actually scored on them (percentage correct) within each area of study, and this paper takes the highest band, with 16 data analysis, 8 recursion and finance, 8 matrices and 8 networks questions. The questions themselves are in the official PDFs (VCAA copyright), so open each one with its link. Answer here, then mark yourself against the guide.

How Further Maths students went on these exact questions: an average of 33 / 40 (82%). Beat that.

Data analysis

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 1 Open in the exam PDF (p. 4) ↗

Difference between the mean and the median of 800 nerve-impulse times, from a summary table

Mean 220 − median 150 = 70 ms.

98% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 1% · B 98% · C 1% · D 0% · E 0%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 2 Open in the exam PDF (p. 5) ↗

How many of the 800 times are longer than 300 ms

300 ms is the third quartile, so a quarter of the values lie above it: 0.25 × 800 = 200.

73% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 3% · B 6% · C 12% · D 73% · E 7%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 5 Open in the exam PDF (p. 6) ↗

Percentage of 20 animal species lighter than 10 000 g, from a log₁₀(weight) histogram

10 000 = 10⁴, so count the bars below 4 on the log scale: 3 + 2 + 12 = 17 of 20 species, which is 85%.

78% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 8% · B 4% · C 5% · D 4% · E 78%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 6 Open in the exam PDF (p. 6) ↗

Which variable a percentage segmented bar chart could pair with month of the year

A segmented bar chart shows two categorical variables. Rainfall recorded as below average/average/above average is the only categorical option.

72% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 17% · B 7% · C 1% · D 72% · E 3%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 8 Open in the exam PDF (p. 7) ↗

Number of 10 000 birds with wing length below 57 mm (mean 61 mm, SD 2 mm)

57 is two standard deviations below the mean, so 2.5% lie below it: 0.025 × 10 000 = 250.

Mean and SD of light-globe lifetimes from two given percentages

50% above 670 means the mean is 670. 16% below 655 means 655 is one SD below the mean, so SD = 15.

77% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 2% · B 8% · C 9% · D 77% · E 3%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Core · Question 23 Open in the exam PDF (p. 13) ↗

How many of four recurrence relations show a depreciating asset

Vₙ − 2500 and 0.875Vₙ both fall. 1.125Vₙ − 2500 stays at 20 000 (interest 2500 cancels the 2500 taken off), and + 2500 grows. Two.

80% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 1% · B 5% · C 80% · D 14% · E 1%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 1 Open in the exam PDF (p. 24) ↗

Number of faces of a connected planar graph with seven vertices and nine edges

Euler’s formula: 7 − 9 + f = 2, so f = 4.

89% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 1% · B 2% · C 5% · D 89% · E 3%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 2 Open in the exam PDF (p. 24) ↗

Which listed route is not a Hamiltonian cycle

DCBAGFED needs an edge from E to D, which the graph doesn’t have. The others visit every vertex once along real edges.

93% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 1% · B 2% · C 2% · D 93% · E 2%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 3 Open in the exam PDF (p. 25) ↗

Which of five trees is not a spanning tree of a given network

Option B uses an edge from 3 to 5, which isn’t in the network. The others use only network edges, reach all six vertices and have five edges.

81% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 5% · B 81% · C 8% · D 3% · E 3%.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 8 Open in the exam PDF (p. 28) ↗

Which pathway network matches an adjacency matrix (including a loop)

Check each entry: a loop at J, 3 paths J–K, 2 J–M, 1 K–L, 2 K–M and 2 L–M. Only E has them all.

75% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 3% · B 9% · C 8% · D 5% · E 75%.

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 1 Open in the exam PDF (p. 22) ↗

Number of vertices of degree 3 in a graph

Count the edges at each vertex: five vertices have degree 3, one has degree 5 and one has degree 2.

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 2 Open in the exam PDF (p. 22) ↗

Which statement about a bipartite graph of friends and the fruit they ate is false

Van’s vertex has two edges (orange and strawberry), so “Van ate only strawberry” is false.

95% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 1% · B 2% · C 95% · D 1% · E 1%.

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 1 · Networks module · Question 4 Open in the exam PDF (p. 23) ↗

Number of vertices of a directed network that cannot be reached from X

Follow the arrows from X: it reaches W, then T, V, U, Y and Z. S only has edges leaving it, so S is the one vertex that can’t be reached.

62% of Further Maths students got this right. Choices: A 62% · B 8% · C 3% · D 7% · E 20%.
