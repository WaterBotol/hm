Question 1: data analysis (12 marks)

Hours studied, x23456789
Test score, y5258616770767985

Q1a. Find the equation of the least squares line for test score against hours studied. Round the coefficients to four significant figures.

CAS linear regression (a + bx) with hours as x. [1]

test score \(= 43.36 + 4.571 \times\) hours studied. [1]

Q1b. Interpret the slope of the line in context.

On average, the test score increases by 4.571 marks for each extra hour studied. [1]

Q1c. Find the coefficient of determination as a percentage, correct to one decimal place, and interpret it.

\(r = 0.9976\), so \(r^2 = 0.9951\): 99.5%. [1]

99.5% of the variation in test score can be explained by the variation in hours studied. [1]

Q1d. Use the equation to predict the score of a student who studies for 5.5 hours, and explain why this prediction is reliable.

\(43.357\ldots + 4.5714\ldots \times 5.5 = 68.5\). [1]

5.5 hours is inside the data range (2 to 9 hours), so it's interpolation (and the association is very strong). [1]

Q1e. Find the residual for the student who studied for 6 hours, correct to two decimal places, and say what it tells you.

Predicted \(= 43.357\ldots + 4.5714\ldots \times 6 = 70.786\ldots\); residual \(= 70 - 70.786 = -0.79\). [1]

The student scored 0.79 marks lower than the line predicts. [1]

Q1f. A teacher uses the line to predict that a student who studies for 12 hours will score 98.2. Give two reasons why this prediction should be treated with caution.

12 hours is outside the data range: extrapolation. [1]

The linear pattern may not continue, for example scores are capped at 100 and gains may level off. [1]

Correlation doesn't show that studying more causes higher scores in general, and the sample is small (8 students). [1]

Question 2: recursion and financial modelling (6 marks)

Q2a. Mia borrows $18 000 for a car at 7.2% per annum compounding monthly and repays $400 per month. Write a recurrence relation for the balance \(V_n\) after \(n\) payments.

\(V_0 = 18\,000\), \(V_{n+1} = 1.006V_n - 400\). [1]

Q2b. Show recursive calculations to find the balance after two payments.

\(V_1 = 1.006 \times 18\,000 - 400 = 17\,708\); \(V_2 = 1.006 \times 17\,708 - 400 = \$17\,414.25\). [1]

Q2c. The final payment is slightly smaller than $400. How many payments are needed, and what is the final payment, to the nearest cent?

N = ?, I% = 7.2, PV = 18 000, Pmt = −400, FV = 0, PpY = CpY = 12: N = 52.6, so 53 payments. [1]

After 52 payments, FV = −242.43; final payment \(= 242.43 \times 1.006 = \$243.88\). [1]

Q2d. Find the total interest Mia pays, to the nearest cent.

Total paid \(= 52 \times 400 + 243.88 = 21\,043.88\). [1]

Interest \(= 21\,043.88 - 18\,000 = \$3043.88\). [1]

Q3a. Kangaroos move between two reserves, P and Q. Each month 15% of P's kangaroos move to Q and 10% of Q's move to P. After the move, 20 are removed from P and 30 are released into Q. There are 600 in P and 400 in Q at the start. Write \(T\) and \(B\) for \(S_{n+1} = TS_n + B\), and find \(S_2\).

\(T = \begin{bmatrix} 0.85 & 0.1 \\ 0.15 & 0.9 \end{bmatrix}\), \(B = \begin{bmatrix} -20 \\ 30 \end{bmatrix}\). [1]

\(S_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 510 + 40 - 20 \\ 90 + 360 + 30 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 530 \\ 480 \end{bmatrix}\). [1]

\(S_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 478.5 \\ 541.5 \end{bmatrix}\). [1]

Q3b. A different population has Leslie matrix \(L = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1.5 & 0.8 \\ 0.6 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0.7 & 0 \end{bmatrix}\) and initial state 200, 150 and 100 females in three age groups. (i) \(\begin{bmatrix} 1.5(150) + 0.8(100) \\ 0.6(200) \\ 0.7(150) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 305 \\ 120 \\ 105 \end{bmatrix}\). [1]

(ii) \(S_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 264 \\ 183 \\ 84 \end{bmatrix}\), total 531. [1]

Q4a. Find the earliest starting time of E and the minimum completion time of the project.

EST of E \(= \max(0 + 5, 3 + 2) = 5\) days. [1]

G starts at \(\max(5 + 6, 7 + 3) = 11\); completion \(= 11 + 2 = 13\) days. [1]

Q4b. This project has two critical paths. Write them down, and find the float of activity F.

A–C–E–G and B–E–G (both 13 days). [1]

LST of F \(= 11 - 3 = 8\), EST \(= 7\): float = 1 day. [1]

Q4c. Activity E is reduced from 6 days to 4 days. Find the new minimum completion time and explain why it falls by less than 2 days.

Paths: A–C–E–G = 11, B–E–G = 11, A–D–F–G = \(3 + 4 + 3 + 2 = 12\). New time: 12 days. [1]

A–D–F–G (12 days) doesn't use E, so it becomes the critical path and limits the saving to 1 day. [1]
