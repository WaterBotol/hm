Every path is a trail and every trail is a walk. Every cycle is a circuit. A path can't repeat vertices, which automatically means it can't repeat edges.

Eulerian trails and circuits: every edge once

An Eulerian trail uses every edge exactly once. An Eulerian circuit does the same and finishes where it started. Whether one exists depends only on the degrees.

Conditions (graph must be connected)
\[ \text{all vertices even} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{Eulerian circuit (start anywhere)} \]\[ \text{exactly two odd vertices} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{Eulerian trail (start at one odd vertex, finish at the other)} \]

More than two odd vertices: neither exists.

Hamiltonian paths and cycles: every vertex once

A Hamiltonian path visits every vertex exactly once. A Hamiltonian cycle also returns to its start. There's no degree rule: find them by trial.

A courier must visit every town above exactly once and return to P. Find a route and its length.

That's a Hamiltonian cycle. P has only two roads (to Q and R), so the cycle must use both.

P–Q–S–T–R–P uses roads 4, 8, 2, 7 and 5. (The reverse, P–R–T–S–Q–P, is the same cycle.)

P–Q–S–T–R–P, 26 km. It doesn't need every road (QR and RS are unused).

Eulerian: every edge once (think “E for edge”). Hamiltonian: every vertex once; edges can be skipped. Collecting rubbish down every street is Eulerian; visiting every shop is Hamiltonian.

Give routes as a sequence of vertices (e.g. Q–P–R–Q–S–R–T–S) and count the edges: an Eulerian trail on 7 edges has 8 vertices listed. Check every edge appears exactly once.

Summary

  • Walk: anything goes. Trail: no repeated edges. Path: no repeated vertices. Circuit: closed trail. Cycle: closed path.
  • Connected, and all vertices even: circuit. Exactly two odd vertices: trail, starting at one odd vertex and ending at the other.
  • Visits every vertex exactly once (a cycle returns to the start). No degree rule; find by inspection.
  • Total of all edges + shortest path between the two odd vertices (those edges are repeated).
