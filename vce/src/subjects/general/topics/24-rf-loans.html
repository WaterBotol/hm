The recurrence relation for a loan

Each period, interest is added to the balance and then a payment is subtracted. The balance falls, but only by the part of the payment left over after interest.

Reducing balance loan
\[ V_0 = \text{amount borrowed}, \qquad V_{n+1} = R\,V_n - d, \qquad R = 1 + \frac{r}{100k} \]

\(d\) is the regular payment and \(k\) the number of payments (and compounding periods) per year.

Lee borrows \$20 000 at 6% per annum compounding monthly and repays \$400 per month. Write a recurrence relation and find the balance after 3 payments.

Monthly rate \(\frac{6}{12} = 0.5\%\), so \(R = 1.005\). Apply the rule three times.

\(V_0 = 20\,000\), \(V_{n+1} = 1.005V_n - 400\). \(V_1 = 19\,700\), \(V_2 = 19\,398.50\).

\(V_3 = 1.005 \times 19\,398.50 - 400 = 19\,095.49\).

\$19 095.49.

How each row is calculated
\[ \text{interest} = \frac{r}{100k} \times \text{previous balance} \qquad \text{principal reduction} = \text{payment} - \text{interest} \]\[ \text{new balance} = \text{previous balance} - \text{principal reduction} \]

Early in a loan the balance is large, so most of each payment is interest. As the balance falls, the interest part shrinks and the principal part grows. So the balance falls slowly at first and faster near the end: the graph of balance against time curves downward.

Changing the interest rate

A \$300 000 mortgage at 6% per annum compounding monthly is to be repaid over 25 years with monthly payments of \$1932.90. After 3 years the rate rises to 7%. (a) Find the balance after 3 years. (b) Find the new payment that keeps the loan on track to finish in 25 years in total.

Use Finance Solver twice: first for the balance at the change, then a new loan for the remaining time with the new rate.

(a) N = 36, I% = 6, PV = 300 000, Pmt = −1932.90, PpY = CpY = 12: FV = −282 971.40.

(b) N = 300 − 36 = 264, I% = 7, PV = 282 971.40, FV = 0: Pmt = −2103.68.

(a) \$282 971.40. (b) \$2103.68 per month (about \$171 more). If the payment stayed at \$1932.90, the remaining 264 months would stretch to about 331 months.

Extra repayments and interest-only loans

Instead of \$1932.90, the borrower pays \$2100 per month from the start (6% p.a., \$300 000). How many payments are needed and how much interest is saved? (On the original plan, total interest is \$279 870.)

Solve for N, then work out the final payment and total paid.

N = 251.2: 251 payments of \$2100 then a final payment of \$375.52 (from the balance after 251 payments).

Total paid: \(251 \times 2100 + 375.52 = 527\,475.52\); interest \(= 227\,475.52\).

252 payments (about 4 years sooner) and \$52 394.48 less interest.

The payment equals the interest each period, so the balance never falls: \(V_{n+1} = R\,V_n - d\) with \(d = \frac{r}{100k}V_0\). For \$400 000 at 6.6% per annum, monthly payments are \(\frac{0.066}{12} \times 400\,000 = \$2200\), and the full \$400 000 is still owed at the end.

Questions often say “the final repayment is slightly different from the others”. Solve for N, use the whole number of full payments to find the remaining balance, and calculate the last payment. Don't assume every payment is equal.

Practice

A loan of \$15 000 at 8.4% per annum compounding monthly is repaid at \$350 per month. The recurrence relation is

  1. \(V_{n+1} = 1.084V_n - 350\)
  2. \(V_{n+1} = 1.007V_n + 350\)
  3. \(V_{n+1} = 1.007V_n - 350\)
  4. \(V_{n+1} = 0.993V_n - 350\)

\(\frac{8.4}{12} = 0.7\%\) per month: \(R = 1.007\); payments are subtracted.

The interest in the first month of that loan is

  1. \$105
  2. \$1260
  3. \$350
  4. \$245

\(0.007 \times 15\,000 = 105\).

In an amortisation table for a reducing balance loan, as time goes on

  1. the interest part of each payment increases
  2. the payment increases
  3. the principal part of each payment decreases
  4. the principal part of each payment increases

The balance falls, so interest falls and more of each (fixed) payment reduces the principal.

A loan is modelled by \(V_0 = 80\,000\), \(V_{n+1} = 1.005V_n - 400\). This is

  1. a reducing balance loan that is paid off in about 20 years
  2. an interest-only loan
  3. an annuity investment
  4. a loan whose balance grows

Interest each month is \(0.005 \times 80\,000 = 400\), exactly the payment, so the balance stays at \$80 000.

Summary

  • \(V_0 = \) amount borrowed, \(V_{n+1} = RV_n - d\), with \(R = 1 + \frac{r}{100k}\).
  • Interest = periodic rate × previous balance; principal reduction = payment − interest; new balance = previous balance − principal reduction.
  • Find the balance at the change (solve FV), then treat it as a new loan with the new rate and remaining time.
  • Payments equal the interest, so the balance stays the same. Extra repayments shorten the loan and reduce total interest.
