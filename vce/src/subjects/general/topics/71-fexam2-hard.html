General Mathematics Exam 2 · Further Maths questions, harder

These are real VCAA questions from the 2020–2022 Further Mathematics exams, the subject General Maths replaced in 2023. Only the core (data analysis, recursion and finance) and the Matrices and Networks modules are used, and questions on content that isn’t in the current General Maths study design (solving simultaneous equations with matrices) are left out. They were ranked by how Further Maths students actually scored on them (state average ÷ marks) within each area of study, and this paper takes the lowest band, with 24 marks of data analysis, 12 of recursion and finance, 10 of matrices and 12 of networks. The questions themselves are in the official PDFs (VCAA copyright), so open each one with its link. Answer here, then mark yourself against the guide.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Core · Question 5 Open in the exam PDF (p. 10) ↗

Body density against waist measurement for 250 men: drawing, using and checking a given least squares line

State average for the whole question: 3.3 / 7 (47%).

Draw the least squares line on the scatterplot

  • Straight line through two correctly calculated points across the data

Calculate two points, e.g. waist 60 cm gives 1.104 and 130 cm gives 0.998, and join them with a ruler. Lines drawn by eye didn’t score.

State average 0.4 / 1 (40%); 41% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 59% · 1: 41%

Predicted body density for a 65 cm waist, to two decimal places

  • 1.10 kg/L

1.195 − 0.001512 × 65 = 1.097 ≈ 1.10.

State average 0.7 / 1 (70%); 73% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 27% · 1: 73%

Is that prediction extrapolation or interpolation?

  • extrapolating

Extrapolating: 65 cm is outside the range of the waist data, even though it is on the axes.

State average 0.4 / 1 (40%); 38% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 62% · 1: 38%

Interpret the slope

  • On average, body density decreases by 0.001512 kg/L for each 1 cm increase in waist measurement

Body density decreases by 0.001512 kg/L, on average, for each 1 cm increase in waist. “Decreases by −0.001512” or leaving out “each 1 cm” lost the mark.

State average 0.4 / 1 (40%); 44% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 56% · 1: 44%

Show that the residual for a 122 cm waist and density 0.995 is −0.02

  • 0.995 − (1.195 − 0.001512 × 122) = −0.0155 ≈ −0.02

Predicted = 1.0105, and residual = actual − predicted = 0.995 − 1.0105 = −0.0155, which rounds to −0.02.

State average 0.5 / 1 (50%); 51% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 49% · 1: 51%

Correlation coefficient from r² = 0.6783, to three decimal places

  • −0.824

r = −√0.6783 = −0.824. It is negative because the slope is. Most students missed the sign.

State average 0.3 / 1 (30%); 25% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 75% · 1: 25%

Does the residual plot support linearity? Explain

  • Yes: the residuals show no clear pattern (randomly scattered)

Yes: the residuals are scattered randomly with no clear pattern.

State average 0.6 / 1 (60%); 59% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 41% · 1: 59%

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Core · Question 6 Open in the exam PDF (p. 12) ↗

Mean height against mean age for seven age groups of women: a line through two points, then a transformation to linearise

State average for the whole question: 2.2 / 5 (44%).

Difference between the mean heights of women in their twenties and their eighties

  • 10.4 cm

167.1 − 156.7 = 10.4 cm.

State average 0.9 / 1 (90%); 87% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 13% · 1: 87%

Describe the association: strength and direction

  • strong, negative

Strong and negative. Extra comments that contradicted this lost the mark.

State average 0.5 / 1 (50%); 51% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 49% · 1: 51%

Equation of the line through (20, 168) and (85, 157), to three significant figures

  • mean height = 171 − 0.169 × mean age

Slope = (157 − 168) ÷ (85 − 20) = −0.169; intercept = 168 + 0.169 × 20 ≈ 171. Many used CAS on the whole table instead of the two given points.

State average 0.2 / 1 (20%); 23% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 77% · 1: 23%

Transform mean age to linearise the data and fit a least squares line, to four significant figures

  • Uses a squared transformation of mean age
  • mean height = 167.9 − 0.001621 × (mean age)²

The data curve downwards, so square mean age. CAS on (age², height) gives mean height = 167.9 − 0.001621 × (mean age)². Few rounded to four significant figures correctly.

State average 0.6 / 2 (30%); 21% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 62% · 1: 17% · 2: 21%

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Core · Question 1 Open in the exam PDF (p. 2) ↗

High jump, shot-put and javelin results for 15 heptathletes: variables, mean, z-scores, the normal model and boxplots

State average for the whole question: 3.9 / 7 (56%).

Number of numerical variables in the table

  • 3

Three (high jump, shot-put and javelin); athlete number is a label. Many counted the 45 values instead.

State average 0.5 / 1 (50%); 52% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 48% · 1: 52%

Mean high jump height

  • 1.81 m

27.15 ÷ 15 = 1.81 m.

State average 0.9 / 1 (90%); 87% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 13% · 1: 87%

Standardised score for a 14.50 m shot-put throw (mean 13.74, SD 1.43), to one decimal place

  • 0.5

z = (14.50 − 13.74) ÷ 1.43 ≈ 0.53, so 0.5.

State average 0.8 / 1 (80%); 78% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 22% · 1: 78%

Percentage expected to jump higher than an athlete with z = −1.0

  • 84%

16% lie below z = −1, so 84% jump higher. 16% was the common wrong answer.

State average 0.6 / 1 (60%); 60% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 40% · 1: 60%

Why the high jump boxplot has no upper whisker

  • The maximum (1.87) equals Q3

Q3 and the maximum are both 1.87 m (four athletes jumped 1.87), so there is nothing to draw above the box.

State average 0.5 / 1 (50%); 47% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 53% · 1: 47%

Minimum javelin throw that would show as an upper outlier

  • Q1 = 40.88 and Q3 = 42.88, so upper fence = 42.88 + 1.5 × 2 = 45.88
  • More than 45.88 m, so 45.89 m

An outlier must be greater than the fence of 45.88 m, so the minimum is 45.89 m. Most gave 45.88.

State average 0.6 / 2 (30%); 9% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 49% · 1: 42% · 2: 9%

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Core · Question 4 Open in the exam PDF (p. 8) ↗

Men’s and women’s 100 m freestyle winning times since 1912, each with a least squares trend line

State average for the whole question: 2.9 / 5 (58%).

Draw the women’s least squares line on the plot

  • Straight line through two correctly calculated points, e.g. (1908, 75.3) and (2020, 48.0)

Work out two points from 538.9 − 0.2430 × year, such as 75.256 in 1908 and 48.04 in 2020, and join them.

State average 0.5 / 1 (50%); 46% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 54% · 1: 46%

Predicted women’s minus men’s winning time for 2024, to one decimal place

  • Women 47.068 and men 44.394 (or equivalent working)
  • 2.7 seconds

(538.9 − 0.2430 × 2024) − (356.9 − 0.1544 × 2024) = 2.67 ≈ 2.7 s. Rounding too early was the main error.

State average 1.5 / 2 (75%); 67% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 20% · 1: 12% · 2: 67%

First Olympic year the lines predict women will be faster than men

  • Lines cross at about 2054.2
  • 2056

Solve 538.9 − 0.2430x = 356.9 − 0.1544x: x ≈ 2054.2. The next Olympic year after that is 2056.

State average 0.9 / 2 (45%); 40% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 57% · 1: 4% · 2: 40%

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Core · Question 11 Open in the exam PDF (p. 18) ↗

A loan at 4.1% p.a. with known balances after four and seven years: how many more repayments are needed

State average for the whole question: 0.4 / 2 (20%).

Number of further monthly repayments after the first seven years

  • Finds the monthly repayment of $2400
  • 150 more repayments

Finance Solver from year 4 to year 7: N = 36, PV = 329 587.25, FV = −280 875.15 gives PMT = −2400. Then PV = 280 875.15, PMT = −2400, FV = 0 gives N ≈ 149.7, so 150 more (the last one smaller). Entering PV and FV with the same sign was a common error.

State average 0.4 / 2 (20%); 19% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 77% · 1: 3% · 2: 19%

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Core · Question 8 Open in the exam PDF (p. 13) ↗

A $570 000 loan with fortnightly interest: S₀ = 570 000, Sₙ₊₁ = 1.001Sₙ − 1193

State average for the whole question: 1.2 / 3 (40%).

Balance after the first repayment

  • $569 377

1.001 × 570 000 − 1193 = $569 377.

State average 0.7 / 1 (70%); 68% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 32% · 1: 68%

Show the interest rate is 2.6% p.a.

  • (1.001 − 1) × 26 × 100 = 2.6

0.1% a fortnight × 26 fortnights = 2.6% p.a. CAS syntax doesn’t count as showing.

State average 0.3 / 1 (30%); 27% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 73% · 1: 27%

Final (larger) repayment that clears the loan, to the nearest cent

  • $1198.59

Finance Solver gives N ≈ 650.005, so 650 payments with the last one larger. The balance after 649 payments, plus a fortnight’s interest, is $1198.59. $1197.39 left out that interest.

State average 0.2 / 1 (20%); 15% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 85% · 1: 15%

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Core · Question 9 Open in the exam PDF (p. 14) ↗

A $152 431 annuity paying $900 a month for 25 years at 5.1% p.a., then a rate cut after two years

State average for the whole question: 0.8 / 3 (27%).

Value after two months, showing recursive calculations

  • Factor 1.00425
  • V₁ = 152 178.83 and V₂ = 151 925.59

V₁ = 1.00425 × 152 431 − 900 = 152 178.83; V₂ = 1.00425 × 152 178.83 − 900 = $151 925.59. Common errors: adding the 900, or not showing both steps.

State average 0.7 / 2 (35%); 21% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 58% · 1: 21% · 2: 21%

One-off amount to add after two years so the payments last the same time at 4.6% p.a.

  • $7039.20

After 24 payments the balance is $146 073.74. At 4.6%, 276 more payments of $900 need $153 112.94. The difference is $7039.20. Only 10% of marks were earned here.

State average 0.1 / 1 (10%); 8% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 92% · 1: 8%

A $580 000 loan modelled by L₀ = 580 000, Lₙ₊₁ = 1.002Lₙ − 3045.26

Balance after two months, showing recursive calculations

  • L₁ = 578 114.74 and L₂ = 576 225.71

L₁ = 1.002 × 580 000 − 3045.26 = 578 114.74; L₂ = 1.002 × 578 114.74 − 3045.26 = $576 225.71.

State average 0.5 / 1 (50%); 44% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 56% · 1: 44%

Annual interest rate

  • 2.4%

0.2% a month × 12 = 2.4% p.a. 0.2% was a common answer.

Mark distribution: 0: 49% · 1: 51%

Amount by which the final repayment is smaller, to the nearest cent

  • $0.15

Finance Solver: N ≈ 239.99, so 240 payments. With N = 240 the balance overshoots by FV ≈ 0.15, so the last payment is $0.15 smaller.

Multiplying factor that would repay the loan one year sooner, to four decimal places

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Matrices module · Question 3 Open in the exam PDF (p. 18) ↗

Monthly switching between three olive oil brands for 8000 shoppers, then a model with extra shoppers added each month

State average for the whole question: 1.7 / 5 (34%).

How many shoppers bought a different brand in August from July

  • 1160

Stayers = 0.85 × 3200 + 0.80 × 2000 + 0.90 × 2800 = 6840, so 8000 − 6840 = 1160 switched.

State average 0.3 / 1 (30%); 28% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 72% · 1: 28%

Complete S₁

  • S₁ = (3060, 1900, 3040)

TS₀ = (3060, 1900, 3040).

State average 0.8 / 1 (80%); 78% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 22% · 1: 78%

Percentage of August’s Carmani buyers who also bought Carmani in July

  • 89%

0.85 × 3200 = 2720 stayed, out of 3060 August buyers: 2720 ÷ 3060 ≈ 89%. 85% came straight from the matrix and missed the point.

State average 0.2 / 1 (20%); 17% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 83% · 1: 17%

Calculation showing Ohana is bought by 50% of shoppers in the long run

  • Tⁿ × S₀ for large n (e.g. n = 50) gives O = 4000, which is half of 8000

Raise T to a large power and multiply by S₀: Ohana settles at 4000 of the 8000 shoppers.

State average 0.2 / 1 (20%); 22% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 78% · 1: 22%

Value of k in Rₙ₊₁ = TRₙ + B, given R₂

  • 200

R₁ = TR₀ + B and R₂ = TR₁ + B. R₁ = (3260, 2000, 3040 + k), and the Ohana element of R₂ works out to 3262 + 1.9k = 3642, so k = 200.

State average 0.2 / 1 (20%); 15% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 85% · 1: 15%

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Matrices module · Question 4 Open in the exam PDF (p. 20) ↗

A round-robin video game tournament: complete a results matrix from one- and two-step dominance totals

State average for the whole question: 0.4 / 2 (20%).

Complete the results matrix using all the information

  • Rows: I 0 1 1 1 0; J 0 0 1 1 1; K 0 0 0 1 0
  • L 0 0 0 0 1; M 1 0 1 0 0

Katie’s row is given (she beat only Leslie). Ike and Joelene each won 3, Mikki 2 and Leslie 1, and each pair has exactly one 1. The two-step totals (5, 4, 1, 2, 4) then fix the rest: Ike beat Joelene, Katie and Leslie; Joelene beat Katie, Leslie and Mikki; Leslie beat Mikki; Mikki beat Ike and Katie.

State average 0.4 / 2 (20%); 10% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 70% · 1: 20% · 2: 10%

2022 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Matrices module · Question 4 Open in the exam PDF (p. 20) ↗

Votes for three ski captain candidates changing day to day, then a candidate withdraws

State average for the whole question: 1.1 / 3 (37%).

Number planning to vote for Ali after one day

  • 146

Fill in the missing percentages so each candidate’s shares add to 100%: 20% of Lee’s and 30% of Max’s supporters move to Ali. 0.4 × 160 + 0.2 × 140 + 0.3 × 180 = 146.

State average 0.3 / 1 (30%); 32% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 68% · 1: 32%

Who becomes captain after Max withdraws, and with how many votes

  • Uses T⁶ × S₁ with S₁ = (146, 154, 180)
  • Ali with 280 votes

S₁ = (146, 154, 180). Six more days with the new matrix: T⁶S₁ ≈ (280, 200, 0). Ali wins with 280 votes.

State average 0.8 / 2 (40%); 39% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 60% · 1: 1% · 2: 39%

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Networks module · Question 3 Open in the exam PDF (p. 28) ↗

Three training routes through a weighted graph of exercise stations, all starting at S

State average for the whole question: 2.1 / 4 (52%).

Shortest distance from S to O

  • 3.2 km

S–T–U–V–O = 0.6 + 1.2 + 0.6 + 0.8 = 3.2 km. 3.3 km was a common slip.

State average 0.6 / 1 (60%); 58% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 42% · 1: 58%

Name for a route along every track exactly once

  • Eulerian trail

An Eulerian trail (not a path).

State average 0.7 / 1 (70%); 66% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 34% · 1: 66%

Station where that route finishes

  • P

S and P are the only odd-degree vertices, so a trail starting at S ends at P.

State average 0.6 / 1 (60%); 60% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 40% · 1: 60%

Track that must be repeated to visit every station and return to S in the minimum distance

  • S and T

T, M and R each have only two tracks, and all three join S, so a circuit through every station would need all three of S’s tracks. One must be run out and back, and the shortest is S–T (0.6 km).

State average 0.2 / 1 (20%); 25% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 75% · 1: 25%

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Networks module · Question 5 Open in the exam PDF (p. 31) ↗

A nine-activity building project given as a table of EST, durations and predecessors, with one duration missing

State average for the whole question: 1.1 / 4 (28%).

Where the dummy activity goes

  • From the end of B to the start of C

C needs A and B but F needs only B, so B ends at its own vertex where F starts, with a dummy to C.

State average 0.3 / 1 (30%); 26% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 74% · 1: 26%

Duration of activity C

  • 2

C starts at 5 and D and E start at 7, so C takes 2 months.

State average 0.5 / 1 (50%); 49% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 51% · 1: 49%

The four activities that have float

  • A, E, F, H

The critical path is B–C–D–G–I (20 months). The non-critical activities are A, E, F and H.

State average 0.2 / 1 (20%); 23% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 77% · 1: 23%

Minimum completion time if one activity is crashed

  • 17

Reducing B shortens every path through it. Cut B by 3 and B–C–D–G–I falls to 17, while A–C–D–G–I is also 17, so 17 months. Crashing D or G only reaches 19 because of B–F–H–I.

State average 0.1 / 1 (10%); 12% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 88% · 1: 12%

A directed network of stormwater pipes with capacities in litres per minute

Capacity of Cut 1

  • 74

Add only the pipes that cross from the source side to the sink side: 15 + 15 + 14 + 11 + 19 = 74. The pipe from M to L runs the other way, so it doesn’t count.

Maximum flow from source to sink

  • 44

Pipe to reverse for the largest increase in flow

  • From Q to P

Q→P feeds P, which has no way out to the sink. Reversed, it carries flow from N and O through P and Q to the sink: a 6 L/min gain.

Pipe whose capacity should be increased for the largest gain, and its new capacity

  • L to R
  • At least 23
