Fitting the line

The least squares line is the straight line that makes the sum of the squared vertical distances from the points to the line (the squared residuals) as small as possible. Always regress the response variable (y) on the explanatory variable (x).

Least squares line (on the formula sheet)
\[ y = a + bx, \qquad b = r\,\frac{s_y}{s_x}, \qquad a = \bar y - b\bar x \]

With raw data, use your CAS (linear regression, a + bx). With only summary statistics, use the formulas. The line always passes through \((\bar x, \bar y)\).

For a group of adults, arm span (x, cm) has mean 168 and sd 9.2; height (y, cm) has mean 170 and sd 8.6; \(r = 0.92\). Find the least squares line for predicting height from arm span, with coefficients to two decimal places.

Find the slope first, then use the fact that the line passes through the means.

\(b = 0.92 \times \frac{8.6}{9.2} = 0.86\).

\(a = 170 - 0.86 \times 168 = 25.52\).

\(\text{height} = 25.52 + 0.86 \times \text{arm span}\).

If you round \(b\) to 1 decimal place before calculating \(a\), your intercept can be well out. Keep full accuracy (or at least four significant figures) until the final answer.

Interpreting slope and intercept

Use these templates, filling in the variable names, units and direction.

“On average, [response] increases/decreases by b [units] for each one-[unit] increase in [explanatory].”

“When [explanatory] is zero, [response] is predicted to be a [units].” Only say this if \(x = 0\) is a sensible value; otherwise the intercept has no practical meaning.

For 12 students, the least squares line relating test score to hours of study is \(\text{score} = 45.0 + 3.64 \times \text{hours}\). Interpret the slope and the intercept.

The slope is the average change in the response per unit increase in the explanatory variable; the intercept is the predicted response when the explanatory variable is zero.

Slope 3.64: change in score per extra hour. Intercept 45.0: predicted score at 0 hours (zero hours of study is a realistic value here).

On average, test score increases by 3.64 marks for each additional hour of study. A student who does no study is predicted to score 45.0.

Predictions: interpolation and extrapolation

Substitute the explanatory value into the equation. A prediction is interpolation if the x-value is inside the range of the data (reliable when the association is strong and linear) and extrapolation if it is outside the range (unreliable, because the linear pattern may not continue).

Using \(\text{score} = 45.0 + 3.64 \times \text{hours}\) (data collected for 1 to 12 hours), predict the score for 7.5 hours and for 20 hours, and comment on each.

Substitute, then check whether the x-value is inside the data range.

7.5 hours: \(45.0 + 3.64 \times 7.5 = 72.3\). This is interpolation.

20 hours: \(45.0 + 3.64 \times 20 = 117.8\). This is extrapolation, and a score over 100 is impossible.

72.3 (interpolation, reliable); 117.8 (extrapolation, not reliable: the linear pattern can't continue).

The coefficient of determination, r²

\(r^2\) is the square of the correlation coefficient. It tells you how much of the variation in the response variable is explained by the linear association with the explanatory variable.

“\(r^2 \times 100\%\) of the variation in [response] can be explained by the variation in [explanatory].” The other \((1 - r^2) \times 100\%\) is due to other factors.

For the study data, \(r = 0.963\). (a) Interpret \(r^2\). (b) Another data set has \(r^2 = 0.49\) and a negative slope. Find \(r\).

Square \(r\) to get \(r^2\). To go back, take the square root and give it the sign of the slope.

(a) \(r^2 = 0.963^2 = 0.927\), so 92.7%.

(b) \(\sqrt{0.49} = 0.7\), and a negative slope means \(r\) is negative.

(a) 92.7% of the variation in test score can be explained by the variation in hours of study. (b) \(r = -0.7\).

\(r^2\) is always positive, so it loses the direction. When working back from \(r^2\), the sign of \(r\) must match the sign of the slope \(b\).

Practice

The least squares line for predicting fuel used (L) from distance (km) is \(\text{fuel} = 0.6 + 0.082 \times \text{distance}\). The slope means that, on average,

  1. fuel used increases by 0.082 L for each additional kilometre
  2. distance increases by 0.082 km for each additional litre
  3. fuel used increases by 0.6 L for each kilometre
  4. 0.082% of the variation in fuel is explained

Response (fuel) changes by \(b\) per one-unit increase in the explanatory variable (distance).

Given \(\bar x = 20\), \(s_x = 4\), \(\bar y = 50\), \(s_y = 10\), \(r = 0.8\), the slope of the least squares line is

  1. 0.32
  2. 3.2
  3. 2
  4. 12.5

\(b = 0.8 \times \frac{10}{4} = 2\).

A regression of weekly earnings on age has \(r = -0.6\). The percentage of variation in earnings explained by age is

  1. 60%
  2. −36%
  3. 40%
  4. 36%

\(r^2 = 0.36\), so 36%. A percentage of variation is never negative.

A least squares line passes through \((\bar x, \bar y) = (10, 30)\) and has slope −1.5. Its intercept is

  1. 15
  2. 45
  3. 28.5
  4. −45

\(a = 30 - (-1.5)(10) = 45\).

For 10 cafés, the number of coffees sold (y) and the daily temperature (x, °C) give the line \(y = 310 - 6.2x\), with \(r = -0.81\). The data cover 12 °C to 31 °C. (a) Interpret the slope. (b) Predict the number sold at 25 °C. (c) Interpret \(r^2\).

(a) On average, the number of coffees sold decreases by 6.2 for each 1 °C increase in temperature. [1]

(b) \(310 - 6.2 \times 25 = 155\) coffees. [1]

(c) \(r^2 = 0.656\): 65.6% of the variation in the number of coffees sold can be explained by the variation in temperature. [1]

In the café data, a student uses the line to predict sales on a 40 °C day. Find the prediction and explain why it may not be reliable.

\(310 - 6.2 \times 40 = 62\) coffees. [1]

40 °C is outside the data range (12 °C to 31 °C), so this is extrapolation and the linear pattern may not continue. [1]

Summary

  • \(y = a + bx\), \(b = r\frac{s_y}{s_x}\), \(a = \bar y - b\bar x\). It passes through \((\bar x, \bar y)\).
  • Slope: on average, [response] increases/decreases by b for each one-unit increase in [explanatory]. Intercept: when [explanatory] is 0, [response] is predicted to be a.
  • Interpolation predicts inside the data range (reliable for strong linear data); extrapolation predicts outside it (unreliable).
  • r² × 100% of the variation in the response is explained by the variation in the explanatory variable. \(r = \pm\sqrt{r^2}\) with the sign of the slope.
