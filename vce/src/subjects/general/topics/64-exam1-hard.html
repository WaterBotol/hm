General Mathematics Exam 1 · real exam questions, harder

Every question here is a real VCAA exam question. They were chosen by ranking every question from the 2023–2025 exams by how Victorian students actually scored on it (percentage correct), within each area of study, so the paper keeps the real exam’s balance of 16 data analysis, 8 recursion and finance, 8 matrices and 8 networks questions. This paper takes the lowest band. The questions themselves are in the official PDFs (VCAA copyright), so open each one with its link. Answer here, then mark yourself against the guide.

Data analysis

2023 VCAA Exam 1 · Question 6 Open in the exam PDF (p. 5) ↗

How many of six car prices fall in the modal class of a log₁₀(price) histogram

Take log₁₀ of each price: 3175, 4999 and 8925 give 3.50, 3.70 and 3.95, which all lie in the tallest class (3.5 to 4.0).

54% of Victorian students got this right. Choices: A 9% · B 11% · C 54% · D 13% · E 13%.

2023 VCAA Exam 1 · Question 10 Open in the exam PDF (p. 7) ↗

What a negative association (r² = 0.72) between social media time and test scores allows you to conclude

Negative association: less time on social media goes with higher scores. Association alone never shows causation.

59% of Victorian students got this right. Choices: A 59% · B 9% · C 5% · D 22% · E 4%.

2023 VCAA Exam 1 · Question 16 Open in the exam PDF (p. 11) ↗

Seasonal index for a month whose figures are increased by 35% when deseasonalised

Deseasonalised = 1.35 × actual, so index = actual ÷ deseasonalised = 1 ÷ 1.35 ≈ 0.74.

2023 VCAA Exam 1 · Question 22 Open in the exam PDF (p. 13) ↗

Final repayment that exactly clears a $500 000 loan given by a recurrence relation

R = 1.00325 means 3.9% p.a. Finance Solver gives N = 300; the balance before the last payment, grown by one month’s interest, is $2614.12.

2023 VCAA Exam 1 · Question 23 Open in the exam PDF (p. 14) ↗

Effective interest rate from a graph of a quarterly loan balance

First quarter: (19 527.56 + 653.65) ÷ 20 000 = 1.00906, so the effective rate is (1.00906⁴ − 1) × 100% ≈ 3.67%.

37% of Victorian students got this right. Choices: A 22% · B 16% · C 15% · D 37% · E 9%.

2023 VCAA Exam 1 · Question 24 Open in the exam PDF (p. 14) ↗

Expression for R in a perpetuity recurrence relation

A perpetuity’s value stays at a: Ra − d = a, so R = (a + d) ÷ a.

27% of Victorian students got this right. Choices: A 4% · B 27% · C 10% · D 37% · E 21%.

2024 VCAA Exam 1 · Question 17 Open in the exam PDF (p. 11) ↗

Values of R and d that generate a geometric sequence

A geometric sequence multiplies by R each time, so d must be 0: R = 2, d = 0.

36% of Victorian students got this right. Choices: A 14% · B 25% · C 24% · D 36%.

2023 VCAA Exam 1 · Question 32 Open in the exam PDF (p. 19) ↗

From an incomplete transition diagram, the percentage of Wednesday’s oval students who were also at the oval on Tuesday

Complete T (columns add to 1), then S₁ and S₂: 140 at the oval on Tuesday and 127 on Wednesday. 0.3 × 140 = 42 stayed, and 42 ÷ 127 ≈ 33%. Using 0.3 itself was the common error.

18% of Victorian students got this right. Choices: A 9% · B 54% · C 18% · D 12% · E 5%.

2024 VCAA Exam 1 · Question 38 Open in the exam PDF (p. 24) ↗

How many of four statements must be true for a connected graph with six vertices and six edges

Only the degree sum (2 × 6 = 12) is certain. A counterexample graph has four odd vertices, no Eulerian trail and no Hamiltonian path.
