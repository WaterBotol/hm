The 68–95–99.7% rule

Many measurements (heights, test scores, masses of manufactured items) have a symmetric, bell-shaped distribution. For these, the mean and median are equal, and the standard deviation tells you exactly how the data spread out.

In a bell-shaped (normal) distribution, approximately:

  • 68% of values lie within 1 standard deviation of the mean,
  • 95% within 2 standard deviations,
  • 99.7% within 3 standard deviations.

Because the curve is symmetric, 50% of values are above the mean. Combine bands to answer any “what percentage” question:

RegionPercentageHow
above \(\bar x + s\)16%\(50\% - 34\%\)
above \(\bar x + 2s\)2.5%\(\frac{100\% - 95\%}{2}\)
above \(\bar x + 3s\)0.15%\(\frac{100\% - 99.7\%}{2}\)
between \(\bar x - s\) and \(\bar x + 2s\)81.5%\(34\% + 47.5\%\)
between \(\bar x - 2s\) and \(\bar x + s\)81.5%\(47.5\% + 34\%\)

The 68–95–99.7% rule only works when the distribution is approximately symmetric and bell-shaped. For skewed data it gives wrong answers, and VCAA will not expect you to use it.

The lengths of a species of fish are approximately normally distributed with mean 42 cm and standard deviation 5 cm. What percentage of these fish are between 37 cm and 52 cm long? In a catch of 400 fish, how many would you expect to be longer than 52 cm?

Turn each length into a number of standard deviations from the mean, then add the band percentages.

37 = 42 − 5 is one sd below; 52 = 42 + 10 is two sd above. Between them: 34% + 47.5% = 81.5%.

Above two sd: 2.5%, and 2.5% of 400 = 10.

81.5% of fish; about 10 fish longer than 52 cm.

Standardised scores (z-scores)

A z-score says how many standard deviations a value is above (positive) or below (negative) the mean. It lets you compare values from distributions with different means and spreads.

Standardised score (on the formula sheet)
\[ z = \frac{x - \bar x}{s} \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad x = \bar x + z\,s \]

A test mark of 72 in a test with mean 60 and sd 8 has \(z = 1.5\). A mark of 76 in a test with mean 70 and sd 5 has \(z = 1.2\). The 72 is the better relative performance, even though 76 is the higher mark.

Mia ran 100 m in 12.8 s (her age group: mean 14.0 s, sd 0.6 s) and did a long jump of 4.9 m (mean 4.4 m, sd 0.4 m). In which event did she perform better relative to her age group?

For running, a lower time is better, so a negative z-score is good there. Compare how far from the mean each result is in the good direction.

Sprint: \(z = \frac{12.8 - 14.0}{0.6} = -2\) (2 sd faster than average).

Long jump: \(z = \frac{4.9 - 4.4}{0.4} = 1.25\) (1.25 sd further than average).

The sprint: she was 2 standard deviations better than average there, compared with 1.25 in the long jump.

Exam marks have mean 64 and sd 9. What mark corresponds to a standardised score of −1.4?

Rearrange the z-score formula: \(x = \bar x + z s\).

\(x = 64 + (-1.4)(9) = 64 - 12.6\).

51.4 marks.

A common question gives a z-score and asks for a percentage: \(z = 2\) means 2.5% of values are higher; \(z = -1\) means 16% are lower. Draw the bell curve and label the bands every time: it takes 10 seconds and prevents most errors.

Practice

Heights are approximately normal with mean 170 cm and sd 6 cm. The percentage of heights above 182 cm is closest to

  1. 16%
  2. 5%
  3. 2.5%
  4. 0.15%

182 = 170 + 2 × 6, two sd above: 2.5%.

A score of 58 in a test with mean 70 and sd 8 has a standardised score of

  1. 1.5
  2. −1.5
  3. −12
  4. 0.67

\(\frac{58 - 70}{8} = -1.5\).

Pulse rates are bell-shaped with mean 72 and sd 6. The percentage of pulse rates between 60 and 78 is

  1. 68%
  2. 95%
  3. 47.5%
  4. 81.5%

60 is 2 sd below (47.5% between 60 and 72) and 78 is 1 sd above (34%): 81.5%.

In a bell-shaped distribution with mean 50, 16% of values are below 38. The standard deviation is closest to

  1. 12
  2. 6
  3. 16
  4. 24

16% below means one sd below the mean: \(50 - s = 38\), so \(s = 12\).

The masses of eggs from a farm are approximately normal with mean 60 g and sd 4 g. Of 1000 eggs, how many would you expect to weigh less than 52 g?

52 = 60 − 2 × 4, two sd below the mean, so 2.5% are lighter. [1]

2.5% of 1000 = 25 eggs. [1]

Josh scored 74 in Maths (class mean 66, sd 5) and 80 in English (class mean 71, sd 6). (a) Find both standardised scores. (b) In which subject did he do relatively better? (c) What English mark would have given the same standardised score as his Maths mark?

(a) Maths \(z = \frac{74 - 66}{5} = 1.6\); English \(z = \frac{80 - 71}{6} = 1.5\). [1]

(b) Maths, since 1.6 > 1.5. [1]

(c) \(x = 71 + 1.6 \times 6 = 80.6\). [1]

Summary

  • 68%, 95%, 99.7% within 1, 2, 3 sd. Bands each side: 34%, 13.5%, 2.35%, 0.15%.
  • 16% above \(\bar x + s\); 2.5% above \(\bar x + 2s\).
  • \(z = \frac{x - \bar x}{s}\), \(x = \bar x + zs\). Negative means below the mean.
  • Only for bell-shaped data. The higher z-score is the better relative performance (for times, lower is better).
