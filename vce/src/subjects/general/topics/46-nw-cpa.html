Precedence tables and activity networks

A project is broken into activities. A precedence table lists each activity's duration and its immediate predecessors (activities that must finish before it can start). In an activity network, each activity is an edge (arrow); vertices are the moments when activities finish and others start.

ActivityABCDEFGH
Duration (days)43376234
Immediate predecessors––AAB, CCD, FE

E needs B and C, but F needs only C. If B and C both ended at the vertex where E and F start, F would wrongly have to wait for B too. So C ends at its own vertex (where F starts), and a dummy (dashed, duration 0) carries C's completion across to E's start. A dummy only shows a dependency; it isn't real work.

Forward and backward scanning

The two scans

Forward scan (EST): start activities have EST 0. For every other activity, \(\text{EST} = \) the largest (EST + duration) of its immediate predecessors. The minimum completion time is the largest finishing time.

Backward scan (LST): for an activity that finishes the project, \(\text{LST} = \) completion time − duration. For every other activity, \(\text{LST} = \) the smallest LST of the activities that follow it − its own duration.

\[ \text{float} = \text{LST} - \text{EST} \]

For the project above, find each activity's EST and LST, the minimum completion time, the critical path and the floats.

Forward scan takes the maximum at merges; backward scan takes the minimum at branches. Critical activities have zero float.

Forward: A 0, B 0, C 4, D 4, E \(\max(0 + 3, 4 + 3) = 7\), F 7, G \(\max(4 + 7, 7 + 2) = 11\), H \(7 + 6 = 13\). Finish \(= \max(11 + 3, 13 + 4) = 17\).

Backward: H \(17 - 4 = 13\), G \(17 - 3 = 14\), E \(13 - 6 = 7\), F \(14 - 2 = 12\), D \(14 - 7 = 7\), C \(\min(7, 12) - 3 = 4\), B \(7 - 3 = 4\), A \(\min(4, 7) - 4 = 0\).

The critical path is the longest path through the network. Any delay to a critical activity delays the whole project. A non-critical activity can be delayed by up to its float without changing the finish time.

Crashing: reducing activity times

Spending money (more workers, overtime) can shorten some activities. Only shortening critical activities can shorten the project, and as you shorten it another path may become critical.

E can be reduced by up to 2 days at $500 per day, and H by up to 2 days at $300 per day. Find the minimum completion time and the minimum cost to achieve it.

Shorten the cheapest critical activity first, and recheck the other paths after each day. Paths: A–C–E–H 17, A–D–G 14, A–C–F–G 12, B–E–H 13.

H by 2 days ($600): A–C–E–H = 15. Then E by 1 day ($500): A–C–E–H = 14, which ties with A–D–G = 14.

Cutting E by a second day leaves A–D–G at 14, so it saves nothing.

Minimum completion time 14 days, for $1100. A–C–E–H and A–D–G are now both critical.

Write EST/LST on the network or in a table. For “latest start time” questions, don't confuse LST with the latest finish time (LST + duration). If a question changes a duration, redo the forward scan from that activity onwards.

An activity has immediate predecessors P (EST 5, duration 4) and Q (EST 3, duration 7). Its EST is

  1. 9
  2. 10
  3. 12
  4. 19

It can't start until both finish: \(\max(5 + 4, 3 + 7) = 10\).

In the project above, if activity D is delayed by 2 days, the project completion time is

  1. 19 days
  2. 15 days
  3. 17 days
  4. 18 days

D has 3 days of float, so a 2-day delay doesn't change the 17 days.

A dummy activity in an activity network

  1. has zero duration and only shows a dependency
  2. is always on the critical path
  3. has a duration equal to the float
  4. can be removed without changing the precedences

It takes no time and exists only so the network shows the correct predecessors. Removing it would change who waits for whom.

In the project above, activity B takes 9 days instead of 3. The minimum completion time becomes

  1. 17 days
  2. 23 days
  3. 18 days
  4. 19 days

E's EST becomes \(\max(9, 7) = 9\), so H's EST is 15 and the finish is 19. B's 4 days of float absorb 4 of the 6 extra days.

(a) J's EST \(= 7 + 2 = 9\). [1]

(b) H's EST \(= \max(7 + 6, 9 + 5) = 14\), finish \(= 14 + 4 = 18\) days (A–D–G is 14). [1]

(c) Yes: the new critical path is A–C–F–J–H (\(4 + 3 + 2 + 5 + 4 = 18\)), so J has zero float. [1]

Summary

  • When two activities share some but not all predecessors; the dummy (duration 0) shows the dependency without adding time.
  • EST: largest (EST + duration) of the predecessors. LST: smallest LST of the followers − own duration (the last activities use completion time − duration).
  • The longest path; its activities have zero float. Float = LST − EST, how long a non-critical activity can be delayed.
  • Only critical activities shorten the project; reduce the cheapest first and recheck paths, as another path may become critical.
