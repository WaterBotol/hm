Why transform?

When a scatterplot (or its residual plot) shows a curve, a straight line is the wrong model. Instead of fitting a curve, we change the scale of one axis so the points straighten out, then fit a least squares line to the transformed data.

TransformationEffect on that axisApplied to xApplied to y
Squarestretches out large values\(x^2\)\(y^2\)
Log (base 10)compresses large values\(\log_{10} x\)\(\log_{10} y\)
Reciprocalcompresses strongly (and reverses order)\(\frac1x\)\(\frac1y\)

The circle of transformations

Match the shape of the scatterplot to one of the four quarters of a circle. The quarter it sits in tells you which transformations to try.

Scatterplot shapeQuarterTry
Increasing, curving upwards (concave up), like growthbottom right\(x^2\), \(\log y\), \(\frac1y\)
Increasing, levelling off (concave down)top left\(\log x\), \(\frac1x\), \(y^2\)
Decreasing, levelling off (concave up), like decaybottom left\(\log x\), \(\frac1x\), \(\log y\), \(\frac1y\)
Decreasing, falling faster (concave down)top right\(x^2\), \(y^2\)

Usually more than one transformation is possible. Fit a line to each transformed data set and choose the one with the most random residual plot and the highest \(r^2\). VCAA questions often give you the \(r^2\) values to compare.

Predicting from a transformed equation

The transformed variable replaces x or y in the line. Substitute carefully, then undo the transformation at the end if y was transformed.

The least squares line for a log transformation is \(\text{score} = 12.4 + 8.6 \times \log_{10}(\text{practice hours})\). Predict the score after 50 hours of practice.

The explanatory variable has been transformed, so substitute \(\log_{10}(50)\), not 50.

\(\log_{10}(50) = 1.69897\), so \(12.4 + 8.6 \times 1.69897 = 27.01\).

A score of about 27.0.

The time (hours) to finish a job is modelled by \(\text{time} = 0.8 + 24 \times \frac{1}{\text{workers}}\). Predict the time for 6 workers.

Substitute \(\frac16\) for the transformed explanatory variable.

\(0.8 + 24 \times \frac16 = 0.8 + 4\).

4.8 hours.

Bacteria numbers (y) after x hours satisfy \(\log_{10}(y) = 1.2 + 0.15x\). Predict the number of bacteria after 5 hours.

This time the response has been transformed: find \(\log_{10}(y)\), then undo the log with \(y = 10^{(\dots)}\).

\(\log_{10}(y) = 1.2 + 0.15 \times 5 = 1.95\).

\(y = 10^{1.95} \approx 89.1\).

About 89 bacteria.

A model is \(y^2 = 4.5 + 12.3x\). Predict y when \(x = 10\), given y is positive.

Find \(y^2\) from the line, then take the square root.

\(y^2 = 4.5 + 12.3 \times 10 = 127.5\).

\(y = \sqrt{127.5} \approx\) 11.29.

After a transformation, the slope describes the change in the transformed variable. In \(\log_{10}(y) = 1.2 + 0.15x\), \(\log_{10}(y)\) (not y) increases by 0.15 per hour. Interpret slopes in terms of the transformed variable, exactly as written.

Practice

A scatterplot is increasing and curves upward (getting steeper). Which transformation is most likely to linearise it?

  1. \(\log_{10} x\)
  2. \(x^2\)
  3. \(y^2\)
  4. \(\frac1x\)

Bottom-right quarter of the circle: \(x^2\), \(\log y\) or \(\frac1y\).

Using \(y = 5.2 + 3.1 \log_{10}(x)\), the predicted value when \(x = 1000\) is

  1. 3105.2
  2. 8.3
  3. 9.3
  4. 14.5

\(\log_{10}(1000) = 3\), so \(5.2 + 3.1 \times 3 = 14.5\).

A model is \(\frac1y = 0.02 + 0.004x\). When \(x = 20\), the predicted value of y is

  1. 10
  2. 0.1
  3. 0.08
  4. 12.5

\(\frac1y = 0.02 + 0.08 = 0.1\), so \(y = 10\). Don't forget to undo the reciprocal.

Three transformations of a data set give \(r^2\) values of 0.912 (\(\log_{10} x\)), 0.958 (\(\frac1x\)) and 0.874 (\(y^2\)), and all three residual plots look random. The best choice is

  1. \(\log_{10} x\), because log transformations are always best
  2. \(y^2\)
  3. \(\frac1x\)
  4. none, because the original data are non-linear

With random residual plots for all three, choose the highest \(r^2\): \(\frac1x\).

The heart rate (beats per minute) of a runner after stopping is modelled by \(\text{rate} = 72 + 95 \times \frac{1}{\text{minutes}}\). Predict the rate 2.5 minutes after stopping.

\(\frac{1}{2.5} = 0.4\). [1]

\(72 + 95 \times 0.4 = 110\) beats per minute. [1]

The value of a collectable ($) is modelled by \(\log_{10}(\text{value}) = 2.05 + 0.031 \times \text{age}\), where age is in years. (a) Predict the value at 40 years. (b) A residual plot of the transformed data shows no pattern. What does this suggest?

(a) \(\log_{10}(\text{value}) = 2.05 + 0.031 \times 40 = 3.29\). [1] Value \(= 10^{3.29} \approx \$1950\). [1]

(b) The log transformation has linearised the data, so the linear model for the transformed data is appropriate. [1]

Summary

  • \(x^2\), \(\log_{10} x\), \(\frac1x\), \(y^2\), \(\log_{10} y\), \(\frac1y\): one axis only.
  • Right side: \(x^2\); left side: \(\log x\), \(\frac1x\); top: \(y^2\); bottom: \(\log y\), \(\frac1y\). Match the curve's shape to a quarter.
  • Pick the one with the most random residual plot and the highest \(r^2\).
  • Find \(\log_{10} y\) from the line, then \(y = 10^{(\text{that value})}\).
