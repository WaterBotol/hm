General Mathematics Exam 2 · Further Maths questions, easier

These are real VCAA questions from the 2020–2022 Further Mathematics exams, the subject General Maths replaced in 2023. Only the core (data analysis, recursion and finance) and the Matrices and Networks modules are used, and questions on content that isn’t in the current General Maths study design (solving simultaneous equations with matrices) are left out. They were ranked by how Further Maths students actually scored on them (state average ÷ marks) within each area of study, and this paper takes the highest band, with 25 marks of data analysis, 12 of recursion and finance, 11 of matrices and 13 of networks. The questions themselves are in the official PDFs (VCAA copyright), so open each one with its link. Answer here, then mark yourself against the guide.

How Further Maths students went on these exact questions: an average of 41 / 61 (68%). Beat that.

Data analysis

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Core · Question 1 Open in the exam PDF (p. 2) ↗

An ordered stem plot of the BMI of 32 men: shape, median and the percentage overweight

State average for the whole question: 2.4 / 3 (80%).

Describe the shape of the distribution

  • Positively skewed

Positively skewed: most values sit between 21 and 25 and they tail off up to 31. Adding “no outliers” was accepted.

State average 0.9 / 1 (90%); 86% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 14% · 1: 86%

Median BMI

  • 24.55

32 values, so the median is the mean of the 16th and 17th: (24.5 + 24.6) ÷ 2 = 24.55. Many gave 24.5 or 24.6.

State average 0.7 / 1 (70%); 70% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 30% · 1: 70%

Percentage of the men with a BMI of 25 or more

  • 37.5%

12 of the 32 men: 37.5%. Rounding to 38% lost the mark.

State average 0.8 / 1 (80%); 84% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 16% · 1: 84%

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Core · Question 2 Open in the exam PDF (p. 5) ↗

A least squares line relating 800 m and 200 m running times, with each variable’s mean and standard deviation

State average for the whole question: 1.1 / 3 (37%).

Round the intercept and slope to three significant figures

  • 0.0393 and 5.28

time800 = 0.0393 + 5.28 × time200. Many rounded to three decimal places instead.

State average 0.5 / 1 (50%); 52% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 48% · 1: 52%

Coefficient of determination as a percentage, from the slope and standard deviations

  • r = 5.2756 × 0.96956 ÷ 8.2910 ≈ 0.617
  • r² ≈ 38%

b = r × s_y ÷ s_x, so r = b × s_x ÷ s_y ≈ 0.617 and r² ≈ 0.381: 38%.

State average 0.6 / 2 (30%); 29% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 67% · 1: 3% · 2: 29%

2022 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Core · Question 2 Open in the exam PDF (p. 4) ↗

Parallel boxplots of relative humidity at 9 am and 3 pm over 30 days

State average for the whole question: 3.3 / 5 (66%).

Is humidity more variable at 9 am or 3 pm? Quote both IQRs

  • 9 am, because its IQR (35) is larger than 3 pm’s (29)

IQR at 9 am ≈ 100 − 65 = 35 and at 3 pm ≈ 80 − 51 = 29, so 9 am is more variable. You had to state the conclusion as well as the values.

State average 0.6 / 1 (60%); 59% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 42% · 1: 59%

Percentage of days with 3 pm humidity below 80%

  • 75%

80% is the 3 pm Q3, so 75% of days are below it.

State average 0.7 / 1 (70%); 70% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 30% · 1: 70%

Complete the five-number summaries

  • 9 am: minimum 40, Q3 100, maximum 100; 3 pm: Q3 80, maximum 100

Read the missing values off the boxplots. At 9 am Q3 and the maximum are both 100.

State average 0.9 / 1 (90%); 90% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 10% · 1: 90%

Do the boxplots support an association between humidity and time of day? Quote a statistic

  • Yes: the median changes with time of day
  • Median 75% at 9 am compared with 63% at 3 pm

Yes. The median humidity is 75% at 9 am but 63% at 3 pm. Quoting the medians without saying they differ lost a mark.

State average 1.1 / 2 (55%); 41% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 36% · 1: 22% · 2: 41%

2022 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Core · Question 4 Open in the exam PDF (p. 8) ↗

Relative humidity against temperature at 9 am over 30 days, with a least squares line (r² = 0.6073)

State average for the whole question: 3.1 / 5 (62%).

Explanatory variable

  • temperature

Temperature.

State average 1 / 1 (100%); 93% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 7% · 1: 93%

Describe the association: strength and direction

  • strong, negative

r = −√0.6073 ≈ −0.78, which is strong and negative. “Moderate” came from using r² instead of r.

State average 0.3 / 1 (30%); 28% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 72% · 1: 28%

Interpret the slope

  • On average, relative humidity decreases by 3.417% for each 1 °C increase in temperature

Humidity decreases by 3.417 percentage points, on average, for each 1 °C increase. Leaving out “each 1 degree” lost the mark.

State average 0.5 / 1 (50%); 44% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 56% · 1: 44%

Residual for the day with temperature 16.3 °C and humidity 45.0%, to one decimal place

  • Predicted = 120.1 − 3.417 × 16.3 = 64.40
  • Residual = 45.0 − 64.40 = −19.4

Residual = actual − predicted = −19.4. Many stopped at the predicted value.

State average 1.3 / 2 (65%); 54% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 23% · 1: 23% · 2: 54%

How Victoria went on this paper

These statistics are the real published results for each question in the year it was set, compiled from the VCAA examiners’ reports for Further Mathematics. A student scoring at the state average on every question would get 41 / 61 (68%) on this paper.

Where students lost the most marks

  • 2021 Exam 2 Core Q2 (A least squares line relating 800 m and 200 m running times, with each variable’s mean and standard deviation): 37% of available marks
  • 2020 Exam 2 Matrices Q3 (Shoppers moving week to week between three centres after one is sold: a transition matrix and a graph of the next 10 weeks): 52% of available marks
  • 2022 Exam 2 Core Q7 (Amortisation table for a $540 000 annuity at 3% p.a. paying $5214.28 a month for 10 years): 57% of available marks
  • 2022 Exam 2 Core Q3 (Eight days of weather data: variable types, median rainfall, and a least squares line for maximum against minimum temperature): 60% of available marks
  • 2020 Exam 2 Networks Q2 (Assigning three players to batting positions 1–3 to maximise their total average score): 60% of available marks
  • 2022 Exam 2 Core Q4 (Relative humidity against temperature at 9 am over 30 days, with a least squares line (r² = 0.6073)): 62% of available marks

What most students got right

  • 2021 Exam 2 Networks Q1 (A house floor plan with five rooms and the outside, drawn as a graph with one edge missing): 85% of available marks
  • 2020 Exam 2 Core Q1 (An ordered stem plot of the BMI of 32 men: shape, median and the percentage overweight): 80% of available marks
  • 2022 Exam 2 Matrices Q1 (Nightly costs of three types of ski-resort accommodation stored as a row matrix): 80% of available marks
  • 2020 Exam 2 Networks Q1 (A graph showing which of five new cricketers had played together before): 80% of available marks
