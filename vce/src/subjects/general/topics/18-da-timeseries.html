Features of a time series

A time series plot graphs a variable against time (time is always the explanatory variable, on the horizontal axis), with the points joined by lines. Use the exact study design terms when describing one.

FeatureWhat it isExample
TrendThe long-term direction: increasing, decreasing or no trendSteadily rising house prices
SeasonalityA regular pattern that repeats over a fixed calendar period (a year, a week, a day)Ice-cream sales peak every summer
Irregular fluctuationsUnpredictable, short-term ups and downs with no patternDay-to-day changes in a share price
Possible outlierA single value well away from the pattern, often from a one-off eventA spike in umbrella sales during a freak storm
Structural changeA sudden, lasting change (a discontinuity) in level or patternA drop in road deaths after a new law

VCAA marks the study design words. “The time series shows an increasing trend and seasonality, with peaks in the third quarter of each year.” Saying “it goes up and down” earns nothing; “irregular fluctuations” and “seasonality” are different things.

Smoothing with moving means

When large fluctuations hide the trend, smooth the series. A 3-point moving mean replaces each value by the mean of itself and its two neighbours; a 5-point moving mean uses five values centred on it. More points means a smoother result, but you lose more values at each end.

Odd-point moving means
\[ \text{3-point: } \frac{y_{t-1} + y_t + y_{t+1}}{3} \qquad \text{5-point: } \frac{y_{t-2} + y_{t-1} + y_t + y_{t+1} + y_{t+2}}{5} \]

Monthly sales are 12, 15, 11, 18, 20, 17, 23. Find the 3-point moving means.

Each smoothed value sits at the middle of its three months, so there is no smoothed value for the first or last month.

Month 2: \(\frac{12 + 15 + 11}{3} = 12.67\); month 3: \(\frac{15 + 11 + 18}{3} = 14.67\); month 4: 16.33; month 5: 18.33; month 6: 20.00.

Smoothed values (months 2 to 6): 12.67, 14.67, 16.33, 18.33, 20.00, which show a clear increasing trend.

Even numbers of points: centring

To remove seasonality from quarterly data you need a 4-point mean (one full year). But the mean of four values sits between two time periods. Centring fixes this: average two neighbouring 4-point means so the result lines up with an actual time period. The same applies to 2-point and 12-point (monthly) means.

Centred 4-point moving mean at time t
\[ \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{y_{t-2} + y_{t-1} + y_t + y_{t+1}}{4} + \frac{y_{t-1} + y_t + y_{t+1} + y_{t+2}}{4}\right) \]

Quarterly sales are 30, 42, 55, 36, 34, 47 (quarters 1 to 6). Find the centred 4-point moving mean for quarter 3.

Find the two 4-point means that straddle quarter 3, then average them.

Quarters 1–4: \(\frac{30 + 42 + 55 + 36}{4} = 40.75\) (sits between Q2 and Q3). Quarters 2–5: \(\frac{42 + 55 + 36 + 34}{4} = 41.75\) (between Q3 and Q4).

Centred value at quarter 3: \(\frac{40.75 + 41.75}{2} =\) 41.25.

An odd-point mean sits at the middle time period. An uncentred 4-point mean sits halfway between periods 2 and 3. Exam questions often ask “which time period does the smoothed value correspond to?”, so line it up carefully.

Smoothing with moving medians

Moving median smoothing is done graphically, with an odd number of points only. For 3-median smoothing, take each group of three consecutive points on the plot and mark the middle one by value (its height) at the middle time. Medians resist outliers better than means.

The values for times 1 to 6 are 14, 22, 18, 25, 19, 30. Find the 3-median smoothed values.

For each window of three, take the middle value when sorted, and place it at the centre time.

Times 1–3: 14, 22, 18 gives 18 at time 2. Times 2–4: 22, 18, 25 gives 22 at time 3. Times 3–5: 18, 25, 19 gives 19 at time 4. Times 4–6: 25, 19, 30 gives 25 at time 5.

Smoothed values at times 2 to 5: 18, 22, 19, 25.

Practice

Electricity use in a house is higher every winter and lower every summer, year after year. This feature is called

  1. trend
  2. irregular fluctuation
  3. seasonality
  4. structural change

A repeating pattern tied to the calendar is seasonality.

The 5-point moving mean at time 3 for the values 8, 12, 10, 15, 20 (times 1 to 5) is

  1. 13
  2. 12.33
  3. 10
  4. 15

\(\frac{8 + 12 + 10 + 15 + 20}{5} = 13\).

Smoothing quarterly data with a 4-point moving mean requires centring because

  1. 4 is too small a number of points
  2. quarterly data have no seasonality
  3. moving means can only use odd numbers
  4. the mean of four consecutive values falls between two time periods

Centring averages two neighbouring 4-point means so the result sits on an actual time period.

After a new freeway opened, average daily traffic on an old road dropped suddenly and stayed lower. This is best described as

  1. an outlier
  2. structural change
  3. seasonality
  4. a negative trend

A sudden, lasting change in level is a structural change (a discontinuity), not a single outlier.

Values for days 1 to 5 are 40, 46, 37, 52, 49. Find the 3-point moving means and say which days they correspond to.

Day 2: \(\frac{40 + 46 + 37}{3} = 41\); day 3: \(\frac{46 + 37 + 52}{3} = 45\). [1]

Day 4: \(\frac{37 + 52 + 49}{3} = 46\). [1]

Quarterly data are 30, 42, 55, 36, 34, 47, 60, 40. The centred 4-point moving mean for quarter 5 is required. Find it.

Quarters 3–6: \(\frac{55 + 36 + 34 + 47}{4} = 43\); quarters 4–7: \(\frac{36 + 34 + 47 + 60}{4} = 44.25\). [1]

Centred: \(\frac{43 + 44.25}{2} = 43.625\). [1]

Summary

  • Trend (long-term direction), seasonality (regular calendar pattern), irregular fluctuations (random short-term changes), possible outliers (one-off events), structural change (sudden lasting change).
  • Average each value with its two neighbours; place it at the middle time period.
  • An even-point mean falls between periods; average two neighbouring means so it sits on a period.
  • Graphically, with an odd number of points: take the middle value of each window at the middle time. Medians resist outliers.
