Simple interest: linear growth

With simple interest, interest is calculated on the original principal only, so the same amount is added every period. It is linear growth.

Simple interest as a recurrence relation
\[ V_0 = P, \qquad V_{n+1} = V_n + d, \qquad d = \frac{r}{100} \times P \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad V_n = P + nd \]

\$8000 is invested at 3.5% per annum simple interest. Write a recurrence relation and find the value after 5 years.

The yearly interest is always 3.5% of the original \$8000.

\(d = 0.035 \times 8000 = 280\): \(V_0 = 8000\), \(V_{n+1} = V_n + 280\).

\(V_5 = 8000 + 5 \times 280 = 9400\).

\$9400.

Compound interest: geometric growth

With compound interest, interest is added to the balance each compounding period, and the next period's interest is calculated on the new, larger balance. An annual (nominal) rate of \(r\%\) compounded \(k\) times a year means \(\frac{r}{k}\%\) per period.

Compound interest
\[ V_0 = P, \qquad V_{n+1} = R\,V_n, \qquad R = 1 + \frac{r}{100k} \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad V_n = R^n V_0 \]

\(r\) = annual rate, \(k\) = compounding periods per year (12 monthly, 4 quarterly, 365 daily, 26 fortnightly), \(n\) = number of compounding periods.

\$5000 is invested at 4.8% per annum, compounding monthly. Find the value after 3 years and the interest earned.

Convert to a monthly rate and count months, not years.

\(R = 1 + \frac{4.8}{1200} = 1.004\); \(n = 3 \times 12 = 36\).

\(V_{36} = 1.004^{36} \times 5000 = 5772.76\).

Value \$5772.76; interest \(5772.76 - 5000 =\) \$772.76.

“4.8% per annum compounding monthly” means 0.4% per month, and 3 years is 36 compounding periods. Using \(1.048^3\) gives the wrong answer unless interest compounds annually.

How many months does it take an investment at 4.8% per annum compounding monthly to double?

Generate terms on CAS, or solve \(1.004^n \times P = 2P\).

\(1.004^n = 2\) gives \(n = 173.6\). The balance only changes when interest is added, so round up.

174 months (14.5 years).

A loan with no repayments grows the same way: the debt is \(R^n\) times the amount borrowed.

Nominal and effective interest rates

The quoted annual rate is the nominal rate. Because interest compounds during the year, the actual percentage growth over a year is a little higher: the effective interest rate. Use it to compare loans or investments with different compounding periods.

Effective rate of interest (on the formula sheet)
\[ r_{\text{effective}} = \left[\left(1 + \frac{r}{100n}\right)^{n} - 1\right] \times 100\% \]

Here \(n\) is the number of compounding periods per year.

6% per annum compounding…Effective rate
annually6.000%
quarterly6.136%
monthly6.168%
daily6.183%

For an investment, choose the highest effective rate. For a loan, choose the lowest effective rate. More frequent compounding raises the effective rate.

Bank A offers 4.8% per annum compounding monthly. Bank B offers 4.85% per annum compounding quarterly. Which is the better investment?

Convert both to effective rates.

A: \(\left(1 + \frac{4.8}{1200}\right)^{12} - 1 = 4.907\%\). B: \(\left(1 + \frac{4.85}{400}\right)^{4} - 1 = 4.939\%\).

Bank B (effective 4.939% vs 4.907%).

Practice

\$12 000 is invested at 3.5% per annum compounding quarterly. A recurrence relation for the value after \(n\) quarters is

  1. \(V_{n+1} = 1.035V_n\)
  2. \(V_{n+1} = V_n + 105\)
  3. \(V_{n+1} = 1.00875V_n\)
  4. \(V_{n+1} = 0.875V_n\)

\(\frac{3.5}{4} = 0.875\%\) per quarter, so \(R = 1.00875\).

The value of that investment after 5 years is closest to

  1. \$14 284
  2. \$14 100
  3. \$14 252
  4. \$13 650

\(1.00875^{20} \times 12\,000 \approx 14\,284.08\).

A loan charges 7.2% per annum compounding monthly. The effective annual rate is closest to

  1. 7.20%
  2. 0.60%
  3. 86.4%
  4. 7.44%

\(\left(1.006^{12} - 1\right) \times 100\% \approx 7.44\%\).

The recurrence relation \(V_0 = 2000\), \(V_{n+1} = 1.02V_n\) models an investment with interest compounding quarterly. The annual interest rate and the value after 9 months are

  1. 2% per annum; \$2040
  2. 8% per annum; \$2122.42
  3. 8% per annum; \$2160
  4. 2% per annum; \$2122.42

2% per quarter = 8% per annum. Nine months = 3 quarters: \(1.02^3 \times 2000 = 2122.42\).

Find the effective annual rate for 5.2% per annum compounding fortnightly (26 times a year), correct to two decimal places.

\(\left(1 + \frac{5.2}{2600}\right)^{26} - 1 = 1.002^{26} - 1\). [1]

\(\approx 5.33\%\). [1]

Sam borrows \$3000 at 9% per annum compounding monthly and makes no repayments. (a) Write a recurrence relation for the amount owed after \(n\) months. (b) Find the amount owed after 18 months. (c) How much of this is interest?

(a) \(A_0 = 3000\), \(A_{n+1} = 1.0075A_n\). [1]

(b) \(1.0075^{18} \times 3000 \approx \$3431.88\). [1]

(c) \(3431.88 - 3000 = \$431.88\). [1]

Summary

  • Simple: interest on the principal only, \(V_{n+1} = V_n + d\) (linear). Compound: interest on the current balance, \(V_{n+1} = RV_n\) (geometric).
  • \(R = 1 + \frac{r}{100k}\); \(n\) years = \(nk\) periods.
  • \(r_{\text{eff}} = \left[\left(1 + \frac{r}{100n}\right)^n - 1\right] \times 100\%\): to compare rates with different compounding periods.
  • Investment: highest effective rate. Loan: lowest effective rate.
