General Mathematics Exam 2 · real exam questions, harder

Every question here is a real VCAA exam question. They were chosen by ranking every question from the 2023–2025 exams by how Victorian students actually scored on it (state average ÷ marks), within each area of study, so the paper keeps the real exam’s balance of about 24 marks of data analysis and 12 each of the other three areas. This paper takes the lowest band. The questions themselves are in the official PDFs (VCAA copyright), so open each one with its link. Answer here, then mark yourself against the guide.

2023 VCAA Exam 2 · Question 3 Open in the exam PDF (p. 6) ↗

Ice cream consumption against temperature: least squares line, correlation, interpretation and prediction

State average for the whole question: 3.3 / 6 (55%).

Draw the given least squares line on the scatterplot

  • Straight line through two correctly calculated points across the data range

Calculate two points far apart, e.g. temperature −10 gives 0.1164 and 25 gives 0.2004, and join them with a ruler.

State average 0.4 / 1 (40%); 40% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 60% · 1: 40%

Correlation coefficient from r² = 0.7212, to three decimal places

  • r = 0.849

r = +√0.7212 = 0.849 (positive slope). Some squared r² instead.

State average 0.7 / 1 (70%); 69% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 31% · 1: 69%

Describe the association: strength, direction and form

  • strong, positive, linear

Strong, positive, linear. “Moderate” came from using r² instead of r.

State average 0.6 / 1 (60%); 58% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 42% · 1: 58%

Interpret the intercept

  • When temperature is 0 °C, predicted consumption is 0.1404 litres per person

When the average monthly temperature is 0 °C, predicted consumption is 0.1404 L/person. Many interpreted the slope instead.

State average 0.4 / 1 (40%); 43% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 57% · 1: 43%

Predicted consumption at −6 °C

  • 0.126 L/person

0.1404 + 0.0024 × (−6) = 0.126. Rounding to 0.13 lost the mark.

State average 0.8 / 1 (80%); 75% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 25% · 1: 75%

Is that prediction interpolation or extrapolation?

  • extrapolation

Extrapolation: −6 °C is outside the range of the temperature data, even though it fits on the axes.

State average 0.4 / 1 (40%); 41% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 59% · 1: 41%

Refer to the change in both variables.

Mark distribution: 0: 46% · 1: 54%

Show the residual for 1984 (Wgold 2.02, Wbest 2.07) is 0.0328

2024 VCAA Exam 2 · Question 8 Open in the exam PDF (p. 13) ↗

A $500 000 loan at 5.3% p.a. monthly with $3071.63 repayments: total cost and number of payments

State average for the whole question: 0.6 / 2 (30%).

Total cost of the loan to the nearest cent, and the number of payments

  • 288 payments
  • Total cost $884 633.62

N = 288. After 288 payments FV ≈ −4.18, so $4.18 is still owed and the last payment is $4.18 more. Total = 288 × 3071.63 + 4.18 = $884 633.62. Using 289 payments gave a final payment of about $4, which isn’t “slightly different”.

State average 0.6 / 2 (30%); 13% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 55% · 1: 31% · 2: 13%

How Victoria went on this paper

These statistics are the real published results for each question in the year it was set, compiled from the VCAA examiners’ reports. A student scoring at the state average on every question would get 25 / 59 (43%) on this paper.

Where Victorian students lost the most marks

  • 2025 Exam 2 Q10 (A 10-year, $650 000 annuity at 6.4% compounding quarterly: recurrence relation for the second half): 20% of available marks
  • 2024 Exam 2 Q12 (Construction staff moving between jobs or leaving (absorbing state), then a model with new hires Vₙ₊₁ = RVₙ + Z): 25% of available marks
  • 2024 Exam 2 Q8 (A $500 000 loan at 5.3% p.a. monthly with $3071.63 repayments: total cost and number of payments): 30% of available marks
  • 2025 Exam 2 Q13 (Children switching between cooking, gardening and music each week (transition matrix K)): 30% of available marks
  • 2023 Exam 2 Q7 (A $60 000 holiday loan compounding weekly, Vₙ₊₁ = 1.0015Vₙ − d): 32% of available marks
  • 2024 Exam 2 Q15 (An 11-activity supermarket upgrade network: critical path, float, a dummy, new completion time and crashing cost): 32% of available marks

What most students got right

  • 2025 Exam 2 Q4 (Sale price against distance from the city centre: least squares line, r, prediction and residuals): 59% of available marks
  • 2024 Exam 2 Q3 (Women’s gold medal height (Wgold) and best height that year (Wbest): least squares line, residuals and reliability): 58% of available marks
  • 2023 Exam 2 Q3 (Ice cream consumption against temperature: least squares line, correlation, interpretation and prediction): 55% of available marks
  • 2023 Exam 2 Q14 (A 12-activity renovation project network with a dummy, plus crashing within a budget): 42% of available marks
