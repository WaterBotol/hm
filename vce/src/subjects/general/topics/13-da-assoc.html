Explanatory and response variables

Two variables are associated if knowing the value of one helps you predict the other. In an association, the explanatory variable (EV) is the one that might explain or predict changes in the response variable (RV).

QuestionExplanatoryResponse
Does the time spent studying affect test scores?study timetest score
Is preferred sport related to age group?age grouppreferred sport
Do house prices depend on the suburb?suburbprice

“Does A depend on B?”, “B is used to predict A”, “the effect of B on A”: in each case B is explanatory and A is the response. Put the EV on the horizontal axis of scatterplots and as the columns of two-way tables.

Two categorical variables: two-way tables

A two-way (contingency) frequency table cross-tabulates two categorical variables. To look for an association, convert counts to percentages within each category of the explanatory variable. With the EV in the columns, that means column percentages (each column adds to 100%).

If the percentages are about the same in every column, there is no association. If they change from column to column, there is an association. Here the percentage who exercise falls from 75% to 50% to 33.3% as age increases, so exercise is associated with age group.

40 people aged 30 to 50 exercise and only 45 under 30, which looks similar. But the groups are different sizes (80 vs 60). Only percentages within each group can be compared fairly.

Use the table above to write a response that answers: “Is weekly exercise associated with age group?” Quote appropriate percentages.

Calculate the percentage within each age group and show how it changes across the groups.

Yes: \(\frac{45}{60} = 75.0\%\), \(\frac{40}{80} = 50.0\%\), \(\frac{20}{60} = 33.3\%\).

Yes. The percentage of people who exercise weekly decreases with age group, from 75.0% of those under 30 to 50.0% of those aged 30 to 50 and 33.3% of those over 50.

A numerical and a categorical variable

When the response variable is numerical and the explanatory variable is categorical, display the numerical variable once for each category on the same scale:

  • Parallel boxplots (any number of groups),
  • Back-to-back stem plots (exactly two groups, sharing one stem),
  • Parallel dot plots (small data sets).

“There is an association between reaction time and age group: the median reaction time increases from 250 ms for 18–30-year-olds to 280 ms for 31–50-year-olds and 310 ms for those over 50.” Name both variables, describe the direction of change, and quote the medians. If spread also changes, mention it as a second point.

A back-to-back stem plot shows the numbers of push-ups done by 11 boys (left) and 11 girls (right). The boys' median is 27 and the girls' median is 19. The boys' IQR is 12 and the girls' is 11. Does the number of push-ups appear to be associated with gender?

An association between a numerical and a categorical variable shows up as a difference in centre (and possibly spread) between the groups.

The medians differ by 8 push-ups, while the spreads are similar (IQRs of 12 and 11).

Yes. The median number of push-ups is higher for boys (27) than for girls (19), so the number of push-ups is associated with gender. The spreads are similar.

Percentages within each category of the explanatory variable: the columns.

In a survey, 30% of city residents and 31% of country residents prefer football. This suggests

  1. a strong association between location and preference
  2. more country residents than city residents prefer football
  3. location causes football preference
  4. no association between location and football preference

The percentages are almost the same in both groups, so knowing location doesn't help predict preference.

Parallel boxplots of daily rainfall show medians of 2.1 mm in summer, 4.5 mm in autumn, 6.8 mm in winter and 4.0 mm in spring. Write a sentence describing the association between rainfall and season.

Rainfall is associated with season: the median daily rainfall is highest in winter (6.8 mm) and lowest in summer (2.1 mm), [1] with autumn (4.5 mm) and spring (4.0 mm) in between. [1]

Summary

  • The EV may explain or predict changes in the RV. EV goes on the horizontal axis and in the columns of a two-way table.
  • Find percentages within each category of the EV (column percentages). Changing percentages mean an association; similar percentages mean none.
  • Parallel boxplots, back-to-back stem plots (two groups only), parallel dot plots.
  • Both variables, the direction of the change, and supporting statistics (percentages or medians), in context.
