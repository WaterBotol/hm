The determinant

Every square matrix has a number called its determinant. For a 2 × 2 matrix it is the product of the leading diagonal minus the product of the other diagonal.

Determinant of a 2 × 2 matrix (on the formula sheet)
\[ A = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \qquad \det A = \begin{vmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{vmatrix} = ad - bc \]

Use CAS (det) for 3 × 3 and larger.

The inverse

The inverse \(A^{-1}\) undoes \(A\): \(AA^{-1} = A^{-1}A = I\). Only square matrices can have an inverse, and only when the determinant is not zero.

Inverse of a 2 × 2 matrix (on the formula sheet)
\[ A^{-1} = \frac{1}{\det A}\begin{bmatrix} d & -b \\ -c & a \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \det A \ne 0 \]

Swap the leading diagonal, change the signs of the other two, divide by the determinant.

If \(\det A = 0\), \(A\) is singular and has no inverse. Example: \(\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\) has determinant \(4 - 4 = 0\).

Find the inverse of \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 3 \\ 2 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\).

Check the determinant is not zero, then apply the formula.

\(\det A = 4(2) - 3(2) = 2\).

\(A^{-1} = \frac12\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -3 \\ -2 & 4 \end{bmatrix}\).

\(A^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1.5 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\). (Check: \(AA^{-1} = I\).)

Solving matrix equations

To solve \(AX = B\), multiply both sides on the left by \(A^{-1}\):

Matrix equations
\[ AX = B \;\Rightarrow\; X = A^{-1}B \qquad\qquad XA = B \;\Rightarrow\; X = BA^{-1} \]

Because matrix multiplication isn't commutative, \(A^{-1}B\) and \(BA^{-1}\) are usually different (one may not even be defined). Put \(A^{-1}\) on the same side that \(A\) was on.

Simultaneous equations

A system of linear equations can be written as one matrix equation: (coefficient matrix)(variable matrix) = (constant matrix).

Use matrices to solve \(2x + 3y = 13\) and \(x - y = -1\).

Write \(AX = B\), find \(A^{-1}\), then \(X = A^{-1}B\).

\(\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 13 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}\). \(\det A = -2 - 3 = -5\).

\(A^{-1} = -\frac15\begin{bmatrix} -1 & -3 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\), so \(X = -\frac15\begin{bmatrix} -13 + 3 \\ -13 - 2 \end{bmatrix} = -\frac15\begin{bmatrix} -10 \\ -15 \end{bmatrix}\).

\(x = 2\), \(y = 3\).

Use matrices to solve \(x + y + z = 6\), \(2x - y + 3z = 9\) and \(x + 2y - z = 2\).

Write the coefficient matrix and constant matrix, then calculate \(A^{-1}B\) on CAS.

\(\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 & 3 \\ 1 & 2 & -1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 6 \\ 9 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}\). \(\det A = 5 \ne 0\) (CAS), so there is a unique solution.

\(A^{-1}B\) gives \(x = 1\), \(y = 2\), \(z = 3\). (Check in the first equation: \(1 + 2 + 3 = 6\).)

If the coefficient matrix is singular, the system has either no solution or infinitely many. Graphically for two equations, the lines are parallel (no solution) or the same line (infinitely many).

Practice

The determinant of \(\begin{bmatrix} 5 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}\) is

  1. 14
  2. 26
  3. −14
  4. 20

\(5(4) - 2(3) = 14\).

For which value of \(k\) does \(\begin{bmatrix} k & 6 \\ 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}\) have no inverse?

  1. 2
  2. 3
  3. 4
  4. 6

\(3k - 12 = 0\) gives \(k = 4\).

If \(XA = B\), then \(X\) equals

  1. \(A^{-1}B\)
  2. \(B - A\)
  3. \(\frac{B}{A}\)
  4. \(BA^{-1}\)

Multiply on the right by \(A^{-1}\): \(XAA^{-1} = BA^{-1}\).

The system \(3x + 6y = 9\), \(x + 2y = 5\) has

  1. a unique solution
  2. no solution
  3. infinitely many solutions
  4. the solution \(x = 1\), \(y = 1\)

\(\det = 6 - 6 = 0\), so no unique solution. The first equation is \(x + 2y = 3\), parallel to \(x + 2y = 5\): no solution.

Find the inverse of \(\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 5 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\).

\(\det = 6 - 5 = 1\). [1]

Inverse \(= \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -5 & 3 \end{bmatrix}\). [1]

A café sells muffins ($m) and coffees ($c). One order of 2 muffins and 3 coffees cost $23.50; another of 4 muffins and 1 coffee cost $21.50. Write a matrix equation and solve it.

\(\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} m \\ c \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 23.50 \\ 21.50 \end{bmatrix}\). [1]

\(\det = 2 - 12 = -10\); \(\begin{bmatrix} m \\ c \end{bmatrix} = -\frac{1}{10}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -3 \\ -4 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 23.50 \\ 21.50 \end{bmatrix}\). [1]

\(m = 4.10\), \(c = 5.10\) (muffins $4.10, coffees $5.10). [1]

Summary

  • \(\det A = ad - bc\); \(A^{-1} = \frac{1}{\det A}\begin{bmatrix} d & -b \\ -c & a \end{bmatrix}\).
  • When it is square and \(\det A \ne 0\). If \(\det A = 0\) it is singular (no inverse).
  • \(X = A^{-1}B\) and \(X = BA^{-1}\).
  • Write coefficient × variables = constants and calculate \(A^{-1}B\). If det = 0 there is no unique solution.
