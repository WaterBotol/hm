General Mathematics Exam 2 · Further Maths questions, medium

These are real VCAA questions from the 2020–2022 Further Mathematics exams, the subject General Maths replaced in 2023. Only the core (data analysis, recursion and finance) and the Matrices and Networks modules are used, and questions on content that isn’t in the current General Maths study design (solving simultaneous equations with matrices) are left out. They were ranked by how Further Maths students actually scored on them (state average ÷ marks) within each area of study, and this paper takes the middle band, with 24 marks of data analysis, 12 of recursion and finance, 11 of matrices and 14 of networks. The questions themselves are in the official PDFs (VCAA copyright), so open each one with its link. Answer here, then mark yourself against the guide.

How Further Maths students went on these exact questions: an average of 31 / 61 (51%). Beat that.

2020 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Core · Question 3 Open in the exam PDF (p. 6) ↗

BMI for three neck-size groups of men, given as five-number summaries and parallel boxplots

State average for the whole question: 3 / 5 (60%).

Percentage of the 250 men with a below-average neck size

  • 20%

50 ÷ 250 = 20%.

State average 0.9 / 1 (90%); 93% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 7% · 1: 93%

IQR of BMI for men with an average neck size

  • 2.6

26.0 − 23.4 = 2.6.

State average 0.9 / 1 (90%); 89% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 11% · 1: 89%

Number of the 250 men with a BMI of 30 or more

  • 23

For above-average necks Q3 = 29.95, so a quarter of 76 (19 men) have BMI ≥ 30, as values are rounded to one decimal place. Add the four average-group outliers above 30: 23. Counting the above-average group’s two outliers again gave 25.

State average 0.2 / 1 (20%); 22% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 78% · 1: 22%

Do the boxplots support an association between BMI and neck size? Quote a statistic

  • Yes: median BMI increases as neck size increases
  • Quotes the medians 21.6, 24.6 and 28.1 (or the IQRs)

Yes. Median BMI rises from 21.6 to 24.6 to 28.1 as neck size goes from below average to above average. Stick to one statistic and quote its values from the table.

State average 1 / 2 (50%); 36% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 38% · 1: 26% · 2: 36%

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Core · Question 3 Open in the exam PDF (p. 6) ↗

Women’s Olympic 100 m freestyle winning times since 1956, with a least squares trend line (r² = 0.8794)

State average for the whole question: 3.2 / 6 (53%).

Explanatory variable

  • year

Year.

State average 0.9 / 1 (90%); 90% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 10% · 1: 90%

Correlation coefficient, to three decimal places

  • −0.938

r = −√0.8794 = −0.938: negative because the trend is decreasing. Most left off the sign.

State average 0.2 / 1 (20%); 19% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 81% · 1: 19%

Average decrease in winning time per year

  • 0.1515 seconds per year

The slope tells you directly: 0.1515 s per year. “Write down” means no calculation is needed.

State average 0.3 / 1 (30%); 32% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 68% · 1: 32%

Residual for 2000 (predicted 54.10 s, actual 53.83 s)

  • −0.27

Actual − predicted = 53.83 − 54.10 = −0.27 s.

State average 0.7 / 1 (70%); 68% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 32% · 1: 68%

Show the predicted winning time for 2032 is 49.252 s

  • 357.1 − 0.1515 × 2032 = 49.252

Substitute and show it: 357.1 − 0.1515 × 2032 = 49.252.

State average 0.9 / 1 (90%); 85% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 15% · 1: 85%

Assumption made in that prediction

  • The same decreasing linear trend continues into the future

That the linear trend continues beyond the data. Just saying “extrapolation” wasn’t enough.

State average 0.2 / 1 (20%); 18% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 82% · 1: 18%

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Core · Question 5 Open in the exam PDF (p. 10) ↗

The yearly gap between women’s and men’s winning times since 1912, linearised with a reciprocal transformation

State average for the whole question: 0.7 / 3 (23%).

Fit a least squares line to 1/difference against year, to four significant figures

  • Uses 1/difference as the response
  • 1/difference = −2.234 + 0.001209 × year

CAS on (year, 1/difference) gives 1/difference = −2.234 + 0.001209 × year. Rounding and naming the variables tripped many up.

State average 0.5 / 2 (25%); 16% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 65% · 1: 19% · 2: 16%

Predicted difference in 2032, to one decimal place

  • 4.5 seconds

−2.234 + 0.001209 × 2032 ≈ 0.222, so difference ≈ 1 ÷ 0.222 ≈ 4.5 s. Many forgot to take the reciprocal and wrote 0.2.

State average 0.2 / 1 (20%); 14% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 86% · 1: 14%

2022 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Core · Question 1 Open in the exam PDF (p. 2) ↗

A back-to-back stem plot of daily maximum wind speed in April and November: median, percentages, outliers and a histogram

State average for the whole question: 4.5 / 6 (75%).

Median April wind speed

  • 20 km/h

With 30 values, the median is the mean of the 15th and 16th: both 20 km/h.

State average 0.9 / 1 (90%); 85% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 15% · 1: 85%

Percentage of April days with wind speed below 25 km/h

  • 60%

18 of the 30 April days are below 25 km/h: 60%.

State average 0.8 / 1 (80%); 79% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 21% · 1: 79%

Show that 59 and 70 km/h are outliers for November (Q1 = 22, Q3 = 35)

  • Upper fence = 35 + 1.5 × 13 = 54.5
  • 59 and 70 are both above 54.5, so they are outliers

IQR = 13, so the upper fence is 54.5. Both values are above it. Finding the fence without saying why that makes them outliers lost a mark.

State average 1.5 / 2 (75%); 64% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 19% · 1: 16% · 2: 64%

Histogram of November wind speeds with class width 5 starting at 15

  • Correct column heights 4, 7, 5, 5, 3, 1, 2, 1, 1, 0, 0, 1
  • Columns drawn from 15 to 75 with no gaps except the empty classes

Count each class from the stem plot: 15–20: 4, 20–25: 7, 25–30: 5, 30–35: 5, 35–40: 3, 40–45: 1, 45–50: 2, 50–55: 1, 55–60: 1, then two empty classes and 1 at 70–75. Missing a column or misplacing the 70 was common.

State average 1.3 / 2 (65%); 61% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 28% · 1: 11% · 2: 61%

2022 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Core · Question 5 Open in the exam PDF (p. 10) ↗

A 36-month rainfall time series: features, median smoothing and nine-mean smoothing

State average for the whole question: 2 / 4 (50%).

Two features of the plot apart from irregular fluctuations

  • Decreasing trend and seasonality

A decreasing trend and seasonality. You needed both. Structural change was a common wrong guess.

State average 0.4 / 1 (40%); 40% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 60% · 1: 40%

Five-median smoothed rainfall for month 20

  • 100 mm

Take months 18 to 22 off the plot and find the middle value: 100 mm. Some calculated a mean.

State average 0.6 / 1 (60%); 54% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 46% · 1: 54%

Nine-mean smoothed rainfall for month 7, to one decimal place

  • 133.5

Mean of months 3 to 11: 1201.2 ÷ 9 = 133.5 mm.

State average 0.6 / 1 (60%); 54% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 46% · 1: 54%

Number of points in the smoothed plot if nine-mean smoothing is applied to all 36 months

  • 28

Four months are lost at each end, so 36 − 8 = 28. 4 was a common slip.

State average 0.4 / 1 (40%); 34% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 66% · 1: 34%

Amortisation table for a $320 000 loan at 3.6% p.a. with monthly repayments of $1600

State average for the whole question: 1.5 / 3 (50%).

Principal reduction for payment 3

  • $643.85

1600 − 956.15 = $643.85.

State average 0.8 / 1 (80%); 85% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 15% · 1: 85%

Balance after payment 4, to the nearest cent

  • $317 428.45

Interest = 0.003 × 318 074.23 = 954.22, so the balance = 318 074.23 + 954.22 − 1600 = $317 428.45.

State average 0.4 / 1 (40%); 39% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 61% · 1: 39%

Recurrence relation for the balance Sₙ

  • S₀ = 320 000, Sₙ₊₁ = 1.003Sₙ − 1600

3.6% ÷ 12 = 0.3% a month, so S₀ = 320 000, Sₙ₊₁ = 1.003Sₙ − 1600. Wrong factors seen: 0.003, 0.997 and 1.036.

State average 0.3 / 1 (30%); 30% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 70% · 1: 30%

Shopper numbers at three shopping centres and the proportions in each shopping area, combined by matrix multiplication

State average for the whole question: 3.9 / 6 (65%).

Order of the row matrix V

  • 1 × 3

1 × 3.

State average 1 / 1 (100%); 95% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 5% · 1: 95%

Total Grandmall shoppers if 700 are in its merchandise area (25%)

  • 2800

700 ÷ 0.25 = 2800. Some just copied 2700.

State average 0.5 / 1 (50%); 45% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 55% · 1: 45%

Two missing elements of Q = P × V

  • 1296 and 729

Grandmall column: 0.48 × 2700 = 1296 and 0.27 × 2700 = 729.

State average 0.9 / 1 (90%); 86% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 14% · 1: 86%

Meaning of element q₂₃

  • The number of shoppers (594) in the clothing area of Westmall

Row 2 is clothing and column 3 is Westmall: 594 shoppers in Westmall’s clothing area. Some read q₃₂ instead.

State average 0.7 / 1 (70%); 66% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 34% · 1: 66%

Matrix calculation, using the average-spend matrix, that gives the total of $9663.20

  • [135 143 131] × A₂₀₁₉ = [9663.20]

A row of shopper numbers times the column of average spends: [135 143 131] × A₂₀₁₉. Getting the order wrong was common.

State average 0.6 / 1 (60%); 60% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 40% · 1: 60%

Matrix K that applies changes of +5%, −15% and −1% to the average spends

  • K = diagonal matrix with 1.05, 0.85, 0.99 on the leading diagonal

K must be 3 × 3 so that K × A₂₀₁₉ is a column: a diagonal matrix with 1.05, 0.85 and 0.99 on the diagonal and zeros elsewhere. Few got this.

State average 0.2 / 1 (20%); 24% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 76% · 1: 24%

A model with additions, Rₙ₊₁ = TRₙ + B, for shoppers at the three centres, given R₂

State average for the whole question: 0.7 / 2 (35%).

Expected Westmall shoppers in week 3

  • 237 966

R₃ = TR₂ + B; its Westmall element is 237 966 (B removes 400 from Westmall each week). Say which element is the answer.

State average 0.5 / 1 (50%); 50% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 50% · 1: 50%

Expected Westmall shoppers in week 1

  • 241 000

Work backwards: R₁ = T⁻¹(R₂ − B), whose Westmall element is 241 000. Only 20% of students managed this.

State average 0.2 / 1 (20%); 20% scored full marks.

2021 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Networks module · Question 4 Open in the exam PDF (p. 25) ↗

A 13-activity roadworks network, then crashing selected activities at a weekly cost

State average for the whole question: 0.8 / 3 (27%).

Earliest start time of activity K

  • 14

K needs F (3 + 5 = 8) and J (6 + 5 + 3 = 14): 14 weeks.

State average 0.5 / 1 (50%); 46% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 54% · 1: 46%

Number of activities with zero float

  • 7

Two critical paths of 19 weeks: A–D–G–L–M and A–E–H–L–M. That makes A, D, E, G, H, L and M critical: seven. Many found only one path.

State average 0.2 / 1 (20%); 22% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 78% · 1: 22%

Minimum cost of reducing the completion time to 16 weeks

  • $380 000

L is on both critical paths: cut it 2 weeks ($240 000). A–E–J–K (17 weeks) then also needs cutting, so cut A by 1 week ($140 000), which shortens every long path. Total $380 000.

State average 0.1 / 1 (10%); 9% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 91% · 1: 9%

2022 VCAA Further Maths Exam 2 · Networks module · Question 2 Open in the exam PDF (p. 23) ↗

A 10-activity project network for building a new cabin, then adding the cabin to the road network from an adjacency matrix

State average for the whole question: 3.2 / 5 (64%).

Number of activities with two immediate predecessors

  • 3

H and I both follow D and F, and J follows G and H: three. 2 was the common error.

State average 0.5 / 1 (50%); 49% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 51% · 1: 49%

Minimum completion time

  • 30 weeks

Two critical paths of 30 weeks: A–C–F–I and A–D–I.

State average 0.6 / 1 (60%); 62% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 38% · 1: 62%

Two activities to reduce (by 2 weeks and 1 week) to cut the completion time by three weeks

  • A reduced by 2 weeks and I reduced by 1 week

A is on every path, so cutting it 2 weeks saves 2. Then I is on both 28-week critical paths; cutting it 1 week brings everything down to 27 (A–C–F–H–J and A–D–H–J are also 27).

State average 0.3 / 1 (30%); 27% scored full marks.

Mark distribution: 0: 73% · 1: 27%

Add the new cabin H and its roads using the adjacency matrix

  • Vertex H joined to D and to E
  • A loop at H

Row H has 1s under D, E and H: draw H with edges to D and E and a loop.

State average 1.8 / 2 (90%); 80% scored full marks.

How Victoria went on this paper

These statistics are the real published results for each question in the year it was set, compiled from the VCAA examiners’ reports for Further Mathematics. A student scoring at the state average on every question would get 31 / 61 (51%) on this paper.

Where students lost the most marks

  • 2021 Exam 2 Core Q5 (The yearly gap between women’s and men’s winning times since 1912, linearised with a reciprocal transformation): 23% of available marks
  • 2021 Exam 2 Networks Q3 (A directed road network with capacities in vehicles per hour, from the entrance to the exit of a town): 27% of available marks
  • 2021 Exam 2 Networks Q4 (A 13-activity roadworks network, then crashing selected activities at a weekly cost): 27% of available marks
  • 2020 Exam 2 Matrices Q4 (A model with additions, Rₙ₊₁ = TRₙ + B, for shoppers at the three centres, given R₂): 35% of available marks
  • 2022 Exam 2 Matrices Q3 (Students switching each day between beginner, intermediate and advanced ski runs): 37% of available marks
  • 2020 Exam 2 Networks Q4 (A directed network of track capacities, in people per minute, from S to O): 43% of available marks

What most students got right

  • 2022 Exam 2 Core Q1 (A back-to-back stem plot of daily maximum wind speed in April and November: median, percentages, outliers and a histogram): 75% of available marks
  • 2020 Exam 2 Matrices Q1 (Shopper numbers at three shopping centres and the proportions in each shopping area, combined by matrix multiplication): 65% of available marks
  • 2022 Exam 2 Networks Q2 (A 10-activity project network for building a new cabin, then adding the cabin to the road network from an adjacency matrix): 64% of available marks
  • 2020 Exam 2 Core Q3 (BMI for three neck-size groups of men, given as five-number summaries and parallel boxplots): 60% of available marks
